## 7. The verification that mattered most

**Not the one expected.** The draft flagged Article 27(1) - whether the fundamental rights impact assessment duty on credit-scoring deployers survived the omnibus - as the reading to worry about. It survived untouched (`B`). The reading that actually changed the artifact was Article 10(5): the draft cited it for the permission to process special categories of personal data for bias testing, and in the consolidated text it is **deleted**. The permission now lives in a new Article 4a, with a sixth condition, and Article 4a(2) extends it to deployers of high-risk systems and to providers and deployers of any other AI system, while saying in terms that it creates no obligation to test. A draft that had cited "Article 10(5)" from memory would have cited a provision that no longer exists, and would have missed the widening that is the most practically useful change for a lender deploying a vendor's model. That is what reading the held text rather than the remembered one is for.
