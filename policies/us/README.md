# United States - AI Acceptable Use Policy set

**Status: PUBLISHED - verified within its stated scope: the federal sectoral
baseline, California, and Colorado.** Every citation was read in the held
instruments; every other state is named as not held, and the set says so rather
than pretending to be national.
**Educational, not legal advice** - review by qualified counsel before adoption.

## Purpose

An acceptable use policy for AI tools in an organisation subject to US law, with an
enterprise governance annex, built on the federal sectoral baseline (FTC Act s.5,
FCRA, COPPA, GLBA Safeguards, HIPAA), the CCPA and the CPPA's approved
regulations, and the Colorado AI Act as amended. Obligations are stated as rules a
team can apply; provision identifiers live in the research note, not the policy
body.

Focus 5 of the series (implementing and operating).

## What shapes the US edition

1. **There is no single US AI or privacy statute.** The set is scoped - federal
   baseline + California + Colorado - and every other state is named as not held.
   That scope statement is the first substantive thing in the policy, because
   assuming two states cover fifty is the error this edition exists to prevent.
2. **Two different AI tests apply to overlapping ground.** Colorado catches systems
   that are a *substantial factor* in a consequential decision; California's ADMT
   article catches technology that *substantially replaces human decisionmaking* in
   a significant decision. The same tool can be inside one and outside the other,
   so every tool carries two recorded determinations, never one.
3. **A governance framework has legal effect.** Colorado's statute names the NIST
   AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001 in its own text - compliance supports a rebuttable
   presumption of reasonable care, and framework compliance plus discovery-and-cure
   is an affirmative defense. In this jurisdiction the risk program is the
   statutory safe harbour.
4. **The acceptable use policy is itself a statutory condition.** Colorado excludes
   ordinary informational chatbots from the high-risk regime only where they are
   subject to an accepted use policy prohibiting discriminatory or harmful content.
   Keeping this policy enforced is part of what keeps those systems outside the
   regime - a role no other jurisdiction in this series gives the document.

## The commencement finding

Colorado's duties were enacted to commence 1 February 2026; the special-session
amendment struck every operative date for **30 June 2026 - which has passed, so the
developer and deployer duties are in force now**. California's ADMT article gives
existing uses until **1 January 2027** to comply; risk assessments must precede new
triggering processing, with the first regulator submission due by **1 April 2028**.
The set states live duties as live and dated duties with their dates - verified
against the signed amending act and the approved regulation text, not the
reporting.

## Open gates (stated, not hidden)

1. **State breach notification statutes are not held** - no breach deadline is
   stated anywhere in the set; the obligation is named and routed. Obtaining them
   is the recorded next verification.
2. **Other states are not held** - including AI-in-employment and biometric
   statutes elsewhere. An organisation reaching consumers or workers in those
   states must overlay them.
3. **Colorado attorney-general rules (permissive) and ISO/IEC 42001 are not
   held** - the presumption position rests on the statute, and the framework
   position is built on the held NIST RMF.
4. **The CPPA regulations' instrument-level effective date is not asserted** - the
   compliance dates stated come from the held regulation text itself; the Notice of
   Approval remains to be added to the corpus.
