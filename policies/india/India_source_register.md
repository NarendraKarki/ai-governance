## 8. The verifications that mattered most

**The Act commences by its own notification.** The Rules' commencement in rule 1 and
the Act's commencement in G.S.R. 843(E) are two instruments of the same date with the
same three dates. A set that holds only the Rules, as v1.0 did, can say when the rules
bind but not when the Act does, and the penalties are in the Act. Both are now held
and both tables are in the research note. Citing one without the other is the most
common error in the commentary in circulation.

**The commencement schedule.** Before the gazetted text was read, the reasonable
assumption about a rulebook published in November 2025 is that it binds. It does not.
Rules 1(2) to (4) stage commencement across three dates, and **the stage carrying every
operating obligation - notice, security safeguards, breach intimation, erasure,
rights, transfers, and the Significant Data Fiduciary duties - lands on 13 May 2027**,
255 days from today. What is live today is the construction of the regulator.

A set written on the assumption that the Rules bind would have told an organisation to
do, this quarter, a dozen things it has eighteen months to prepare - and would have
missed the point that actually matters, which is that eighteen months is shorter than
the life of an AI contract signed now. Policy 4.4 and annex A3.5 carry that.

**The direction of the retention obligation.** Rule 8(3) and rule 6(1)(e) each impose a
**one-year minimum** retention period on personal data, associated traffic data and
processing logs - rule 8(3) on every Data Fiduciary, without qualification, and
expressly reaching processing carried out by a Data Processor on its behalf. The Rules'
own illustration confirms it against a cloud provider.

This is the opposite of what a privacy policy usually says, and it is the finding most
likely to be got backwards by a team applying instincts formed elsewhere. It has a
direct and counter-intuitive consequence for AI procurement: **a supplier's
zero-retention or short-retention mode, marketed as a privacy feature, cannot be used
for personal data.** Policy 3.6, 6.5 and Section 12, and annex A6.4 and A7, are all
consequences of that single reading.

**The absence of a breach threshold.** Rule 7(1) attaches on becoming aware of "any
personal data breach" and is owed to "each affected Data Principal". There is no harm
test, no scale test, and no assessment step before the duty arises. That converts
"which of our systems can tell us whose data was touched" from an incident-response
question into a system-approval question, which is where policy 15.6 and annex A2.1
put it.
