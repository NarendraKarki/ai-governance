# India - AI Acceptable Use Policy set

**Status: VERIFIED** against the gazetted Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023,
its commencement notification, and the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025.
Gates that remain open are stated below, not hidden. v1.0 (31 August 2026) was built
without the Act; v1.1 (1 September 2026) adds it, and the research note records what
changed, including one correction.

## Purpose

An acceptable use policy for AI tools in an organisation processing personal data in
India, with an enterprise annex, built from the Act, its commencement notification
and the rules that operationalise it. Obligations are stated as rules a team
can apply; the rule numbers live in the research note, not the policy body.

Focus 5 of the series (implementing and operating).

## Commencement, as verified from G.S.R. 843(E) and rule 1

The Act and the rules commence by separate notifications of the same date, in matching
tranches. Both are needed to say what binds when.

| Date | Act | Rules | Status at 1 Sep 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 November 2025 | Definitions; Board (ss.18-26); powers to make rules and amend the Schedule; consistency with other law | 1, 2, 17 to 21 | In force. No operating obligation on a Data Fiduciary |
| 13 November 2026 | Consent Manager registration (s.6(9)); Board power over registration breaches | 4 | 73 days away |
| **13 May 2027** | Everything else: grounds, notice, consent, obligations, children, Significant Data Fiduciaries, all rights, transfers, exemptions, inquiry, appeals, **penalties** | 3, 5 to 16, 22, 23 | 254 days away. The date that matters |

The policy treats the deferred obligations as design requirements from today, on
the reasoning that eighteen months is shorter than the life of an AI contract signed
now (policy 4.4).

## Levels of use

The organisation's own classification, anchored to statutory triggers: **Open**
(no confidential or personal data), **Internal** (non-personal internal
information), **Personal data** (any personal data; contract carrying the security
safeguards term, lawful basis, recorded retention position, inventory), and
**Consequential** (output materially informing a decision about a person; committee
approval). Policy Section 5.

## Four findings worth knowing before reading

1. **Accuracy is a statutory duty where a decision is involved.** s.8(3) requires
   completeness, accuracy and consistency of personal data likely to be used to make a
   decision affecting the individual. It is the framework's closest thing to a rule on
   automated decisions, and v1.0 of this set could not see it. Policy 7.9 and 10.6.
2. **Retention has a floor, not only a ceiling.** Rule 8(3) and rule 6(1)(e) impose a
   one-year minimum on personal data, traffic data and processing logs, and the duty
   reaches processing by a Data Processor. A supplier's zero-retention mode cannot be
   used for personal data.
3. **Breach intimation has no threshold.** Rule 7(1) attaches on becoming aware of any
   personal data breach and is owed to each affected Data Principal. Knowing whose
   data a system touched is a system-approval question, not an incident-response one.
4. **Children's data carries two prohibitions consent cannot cure:** no processing
   likely to cause detriment, and no tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted
   advertising directed at children. Recommendation and engagement systems are
   behavioural monitoring by design. Policy 9.6.

## Open gates (stated, not hidden)

1. **No order under rule 15 or notification under s.16(1)** on transfers outside India
   is held, and **no specification under rule 13(4)** on data a Significant Data
   Fiduciary must keep in India. The policy states the position rather than inventing
   one.
2. **No notification** of Significant Data Fiduciaries, of an age-based exemption for
   children's data, or of class or transitional exemptions is held. None is assumed.
3. **No sector regulator instrument** is held; no sector overlay exists.
4. Which commencement date the Third Schedule retention period runs from is not
   settled by the text; the research note records the question.
5. The research exemption (s.17(2)(b) with rule 16) is not relied on; its condition
   that data not be used for decisions specific to an individual is noted for counsel.
6. No enforcement decision or case law is relied on anywhere. No provision has been
   reviewed by qualified counsel.
