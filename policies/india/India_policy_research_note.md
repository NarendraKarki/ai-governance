### 1.2 Held since 1 September 2026 - and what changed

**At v1.0 (31 August 2026) the Act was not held**, and the set asserted only what the
Rules require. The Act, its commencement notification and the Board establishment
notification were obtained on 1 September 2026 from the Ministry's own site and read
in full. Policy sections 2.2, 7.2, 7.6, 7.9, 9.6, 10.6, 11.3, 16.6, 17.2 and 22 were
rewritten or added as a result; annex A4.2 and A13 were updated. Section 6 of this
note, formerly the map of the gap, now records what the Act supplied at each point.

**One correction to v1.0 is recorded here rather than hidden.** v1.0 policy Section 10
described all of its automated-decision controls as house standards. The Act contains
a statutory hook this set had not been able to see: s.8(3) requires completeness,
accuracy and consistency of personal data that is likely to be used to make a decision
affecting the individual. Policy 7.9 and 10.6 now carry it as law, and 10.1-10.5
remain house standards.

Citing one notification without the other produces the wrong answer. G.S.R. 846(E)
commences the rules; G.S.R. 843(E) commences the Act. Both are needed to say what binds
on a given date.

What that means in substance:

**In force now** - rule 1 (short title and commencement), rule 2 (definitions), rules
17 to 21 (appointment of the Chairperson and Members through Search-cum-Selection
Committees; their salary and terms of service under the Fifth Schedule; the Board's
meeting procedure, quorum of one-third, casting vote, circulation decisions, and the
six-month inquiry period; the Board's functioning as a digital office; and the terms
of service of its officers and employees under the Sixth Schedule).

**Nothing in that list imposes an operating obligation on a Data Fiduciary.** Read
with G.S.R. 845(E) fixing the Board at four members, what is live is the construction
of the regulator.

**From 13 November 2026** - rule 4 (registration and obligations of Consent Managers,
with the conditions in First Schedule Part A and the obligations in Part B).

**From 13 May 2027** - rule 3 (notice), rule 5 (State processing for subsidies and
similar), rule 6 (reasonable security safeguards), rule 7 (intimation of personal data
breach), rule 8 (erasure periods and the minimum retention period), rule 9 (contact

Policy Section 4 carries this in three subsections, and annex A3 turns it into a
register. Policy 4.4 states the position [ORGANISATION] takes on the interval:
deferred obligations are design requirements from today, because eighteen months is
shorter than the life of an AI contract signed now.

## 3. The findings worth recording

### 3.1 Breach intimation has no threshold, and two clocks run from the same moment

**Rule 7(1)** - "On becoming aware of **any** personal data breach", the Data Fiduciary
shall, to the best of its knowledge, intimate **each affected Data Principal**, in a
concise, clear and plain manner and **without delay**, through her user account or a
registered mode of communication, giving:

- (a) a description of the breach, including its nature, extent and the timing of its
  occurrence;
- (b) the consequences relevant to her likely to arise from the breach;
- (c) the measures implemented and being implemented to mitigate risk;
- (d) the safety measures she may take to protect her interests;
- (e) business contact information of a person able to respond to her queries.

**Rule 7(2)(a)** - on becoming aware, intimate the Board **without delay** with a
description of the breach including its nature, extent, timing and **location** of
occurrence and the likely impact.

**Rule 7(2)(b)** - **within seventy-two hours of becoming aware**, or a longer period
the Board allows on written request, give the Board six further items: updated and
detailed information on that description; the broad facts of the events, circumstances
and reasons; measures implemented or proposed to mitigate risk; findings on who caused
the breach; remedial measures to prevent recurrence; and a report on the intimations
given to affected individuals.

Three things follow that the policy states explicitly:

- **There is no significance test.** No harm threshold, no minimum number of affected
  individuals, no risk gate, and no assessment step interposed before the duty
  attaches. Every personal data breach is intimated to every affected individual and
  to the Board.
- **Both clocks start at awareness.** The seventy-two hours run from becoming aware,
  not from any assessment. Policy 15.5 and annex A8.3 make the recorded awareness
  timestamp the controlling fact.
- **Knowing who is affected becomes an operational requirement of the system, not of
  the incident.** Rule 7(1) is owed to *each* affected individual. Policy 15.6 and
  annex A2.1 therefore make the ability to enumerate affected individuals a condition
  of approving a system for personal data, and A8.4 makes inability to do so an open
  finding.

The extension at rule 7(2)(b) is available "on a request made in writing"; nothing
suggests it is available retrospectively. Annex A8.5 requires the request before the
period expires and treats a late request as a distinct failure.

### 3.2 There is a minimum retention period, and it applies to everyone

This is the finding most likely to be got backwards, because it inverts the usual
privacy instinct.

**Rule 8(3)** - "a Data Fiduciary shall retain, in respect of any processing of
personal data undertaken by it or on its behalf by a Data Processor, such personal
data, associated traffic data and other logs of the processing **for a minimum period
of one year** from the date of such processing, for the purposes as specified in the
Seventh Schedule, after which the Data Fiduciary shall cause such personal data and
logs to be erased, unless further retention is required for compliance with any other
law for the time being in force or notified by the Government."

The subject is "a Data Fiduciary", unqualified. **It is not limited to the classes in
the Third Schedule, nor to Significant Data Fiduciaries.**

**Rule 6(1)(e)** imposes a second, independent one-year floor as part of the minimum
security safeguards: "for enabling the detection of unauthorised access, its
investigation, remediation to prevent recurrence and continued processing in the event
of such a compromise, **retain such logs and personal data for a period of one year**,
unless compliance with any law for the time being in force requires otherwise."

The Rules' own **illustration to rule 8** puts it beyond argument, and its second case
is directly on point for AI supply chains: a company engaging a cloud service provider
as its Data Processor "is required to ensure that the [processor] also retains the data
and associated logs for at least one year before erasure".

Consequences the policy carries at Section 12 and 3.6, and the annex at A6.4 and A7:

- An AI tool configured to purge prompts and logs on a short cycle **cannot be used
  for personal data**. A supplier's zero-retention mode, marketed as a privacy
  feature, is not usable here.
- The one-year floor reaches supplier-side processing, so it is a contract term
  (annex A6.2), not only a configuration.
- Decommissioning a system does not authorise deleting data the minimum still covers
  (annex A5.4).

The **Seventh Schedule** names the purposes the retention serves: use by the State or
its instrumentalities in the interest of sovereignty and integrity of India or security
of the State; use by the State for performing functions or disclosing information under
law; and **carrying out an assessment for notifying a Data Fiduciary as a Significant
Data Fiduciary**. Rule 23(1) allows the Central Government, acting through the
authorised person named in the Schedule for each purpose, to require any Data
Fiduciary or intermediary to furnish information for those purposes; rule 23(2) allows
it to require non-disclosure of that furnishing where disclosure would prejudicially
affect sovereignty and integrity or the security of the State.

### 3.3 The erasure duty, and its collision with the floor

**Rule 8(1)** - a Data Fiduciary of a class specified in the **Third Schedule**,
processing for the corresponding purposes, shall erase personal data - unless retention
is necessary for compliance with any law - after the corresponding time period, if the
Data Principal neither approaches it for the specified purpose nor exercises her rights.

**Rule 8(2)** - at least **forty-eight hours** before that period completes, the Data
Fiduciary must tell the individual that erasure will follow unless she logs into her
user account, initiates contact for the specified purpose, or exercises a right.

**Third Schedule** - three classes, all with the same period:

| Class | Threshold | Purposes covered | Period |
|---|---|---|---|
| E-commerce entity | Not less than **two crore** registered users in India | All, except enabling access to her user account and to a stored virtual token usable for money, goods or services | **Three years** from the date the Data Principal last approached the Data Fiduciary for the specified purpose or exercised her rights, or the commencement of the Rules, whichever is latest |
| Online gaming intermediary | Not less than **fifty lakh** registered users in India | Same exceptions | Same |
| Social media intermediary | Not less than **two crore** registered users in India | Same exceptions | Same |

"E-commerce entity" takes its meaning from the Consumer Protection Act 2019 and
excludes a seller offering goods on a marketplace; "social media intermediary" takes
its meaning from rule 2(1)(w) of the IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media
Ethics Code) Rules 2021.

**Two points the policy carries.**

First, the trigger is a **registered-user threshold that a business can cross without
noticing**. Annex A7.2 requires a written determination of whether [ORGANISATION] falls
within a class, revisited when user numbers move materially.

Second, **the one-year floor and the three-year erasure duty pull in opposite
directions**, and a team resolving the tension informally will resolve it toward
deletion. Policy 12.5 and annex A7.4 require the data protection lead to resolve it in
writing before configuration, and block approval on an unresolved conflict.

**An interpretive question this set does not resolve.** The Third Schedule period runs
from the later of the individual's last contact and "the commencement of the Digital
Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025". The Rules commence in three stages; rule 8
itself commences on 13 May 2027, while rules 1, 2 and 17 to 21 commenced on 13 November
2025. Which date "the commencement of the Rules" denotes is not settled by the text
held. The set flags it rather than choosing, and the difference is eighteen months of
retention.

### 3.4 The one AI-specific duty, and it is narrow

**Rule 13(3)** - "A Significant Data Fiduciary shall observe due diligence to verify
that technical measures **including algorithmic software** adopted by it for hosting,
display, uploading, modification, publishing, transmission, storage, updating or
sharing of personal data processed by it **are not likely to pose a risk to the rights
of Data Principals**."

This is the only provision in the held instruments that addresses algorithmic systems
as such. Three features are worth naming.

- **It is a due diligence and verification duty**, not a prohibition, a classification
  scheme, or a conformity assessment. It asks the organisation to satisfy itself and
  to be able to show that it did.
- **It attaches to Significant Data Fiduciaries only.** The criteria for notification
  are at s.10(1) of the Act: volume and sensitivity of data, risk to rights, impact on
  sovereignty and integrity, risk to electoral democracy, security of the State, public
  order. Policy 11.3 states them.
- **The trigger is the handling of personal data by the software** - hosting, display,
  upload, modification, publication, transmission, storage, updating, sharing - not the
  making of decisions. A model that stores or shares personal data is within it whether
  or not it decides anything.

The rest of rule 13:

- **13(1)** - once every twelve months from notification (or from inclusion in a
  notified class), undertake a **Data Protection Impact Assessment and an audit** to
  ensure effective observance of the Act and the Rules.
- **13(2)** - cause the person carrying them out to **furnish to the Board a report
  containing significant observations**.
- **13(4)** - ensure that personal data specified by the Central Government, on the
  recommendation of a committee it constitutes, and **the traffic data pertaining to
  its flow**, is **not transferred outside the territory of India**.
- **13(5)** - the committee includes officials of the Ministry of Electronics and
  Information Technology and may include officials of other Ministries or Departments.

Policy 11.4 applies 13(3) to every Level 4 system as a house standard, and says so.
Annex A4.4 treats 13(4) as an architectural constraint before it is a compliance one,
because a system that cannot be relocated to India cannot comply with a specification
issued later.

### 3.5 Notice: itemised, standalone, and symmetric on withdrawal

**Rule 3** - the notice given by the Data Fiduciary to the Data Principal shall:

- **(a)** be presented and be understandable **independently of any other information**
  that has been, is or may be made available by the Data Fiduciary;
- **(b)** give, in clear and plain language, a fair account of the details necessary to
  enable **specific and informed** consent, including at the minimum **(i) an itemised
  description of such personal data** and **(ii) the specified purpose or purposes of,
  and specific description of the goods or services to be provided or uses to be
  enabled by, such processing**;
- **(c)** give the particular communication link for accessing the website or app, and
  a description of other means if any, by which she may **(i) withdraw her consent,
  with the ease of doing so being comparable to that with which such consent was
  given**, **(ii)** exercise her rights under the Act, and **(iii)** make a complaint
  to the Board.

Two requirements here are unusually concrete and are the ones AI deployments fail.

**"Itemised description of such personal data"** is not a category label. Policy 7.4
states this and draws the consequence: a notice that does not say what goes into the
AI system cannot support consent for putting it there. Annex A5.2 makes a change that
adds a field or a purpose a change to the notice.

**Parity of ease between giving and withdrawing consent** is expressed in the rule
itself. Policy 7.3 carries it verbatim in substance.

**Rule 9** - every Data Fiduciary shall **prominently publish on its website or app,
and mention in every response** to a communication for the exercise of rights, the
business contact information of the Data Protection Officer if applicable, or of a
person able to answer questions about the processing. The per-response limb is the one
that decays; policy 8.2 and annex A1.3 hold it.

### 3.6 Children and persons with disability - verification, not inference

**Rule 10(1)** - the Data Fiduciary shall adopt appropriate technical and
organisational measures to ensure **verifiable consent of the parent** is obtained
before processing any personal data of a child, and shall observe due diligence to
check that the individual identifying herself as the parent is an adult who is
identifiable if required in connection with compliance with any law, by reference to
(a) reliable identity and age details available with the Data Fiduciary, or (b) details
voluntarily provided by the individual or through a virtual token mapped to them issued
by an authorised entity.

"Adult" is an individual who has completed eighteen years (rule 10(2)(a)). "Authorised
entity" covers an entity entrusted by law or government with issuing identity and age
details or a mapped virtual token, and includes details made available and verified by
a Digital Locker service provider (rule 10(2)(b)). The rule carries four illustrated
cases distinguishing a parent already registered with the Data Fiduciary from one who
is not.

**Rule 11(1)** - where an individual identifies herself as the **lawful guardian of a
person with disability**, the Data Fiduciary shall observe due diligence to verify that
the guardian was appointed by a court, a designated authority under section 15 of the
Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016, or a local level committee under section
13 of the National Trust Act 1999.

Policy 9.3 draws the operational point: these are verification duties and an AI system
cannot discharge them. Age estimation, document classification and identity scoring can
support the check; the determination is a person's.

**Rule 12** with the **Fourth Schedule** exempts sub-sections (1) and (3) of section 9
of the Act - **verifiable parental consent, and the prohibition on tracking,
behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children** - for:

- **Part A, classes**: clinical establishments, mental health establishments and
  healthcare professionals (processing restricted to provision of health services to
  the child, to the extent necessary for protection of her health); allied healthcare
  professionals (supporting implementation of a treatment and referral plan);
  educational institutions (tracking and behavioural monitoring for educational
  activities or in the interests of children's safety); individuals caring for infants
  and children in a creche or day care centre (tracking and behavioural monitoring in
  the interests of safety); and entities engaged by such institutions for transport
  (tracking location during travel to and from).
- **Part B, purposes**: exercise of powers or functions in the interests of a child
  under law; provision of a subsidy, benefit, service, certificate, licence or permit
  in a child's interests under section 7(b); creation of a user account limited to
  email communication; determination of a child's real-time location in the interest of
  her safety; ensuring that information, a service or an advertisement likely to be
  detrimental to a child's well-being is not accessible to her; and confirming that a
  Data Principal is not a child, and observing rule 10 due diligence.

Each is subject to the condition that processing is restricted to what is necessary.
**Because the exempted sub-sections are in the Act, this set cannot state what the
exemption relieves the organisation of.** Policy 9.4 therefore requires a written
determination by the data protection lead against the conditions and forbids assuming
an exemption applies; 9.5 keeps children's data out of AI tools until that exists.

### 3.7 Security safeguards - a prescribed minimum list

**Rule 6(1)** - a Data Fiduciary shall protect personal data in its possession or under
its control, **including in respect of any processing undertaken by it or on its behalf
by a Data Processor**, by taking reasonable security safeguards to prevent personal
data breach, which shall include **at the minimum**:

| | Requirement |
|---|---|
| (a) | Appropriate data security measures, such as securing personal data through **encryption, obfuscation, masking or the use of virtual tokens** mapped to that personal data |
| (b) | Appropriate measures to **control access** to the computer resources used by the Data Fiduciary or the Data Processor |
| (c) | **Visibility on the accessing** of personal data, through appropriate logs, monitoring and review, for enabling detection of unauthorised access, its investigation and remediation to prevent recurrence |
| (d) | Reasonable measures for **continued processing** where confidentiality, integrity or availability is compromised, such as data backups |
| (e) | **Retention of such logs and personal data for one year** (see 3.2) |
| (f) | **Appropriate provision in the contract** between the Data Fiduciary and the Data Processor for taking reasonable security safeguards |
| (g) | Appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure effective observance of security safeguards |

"Computer resource" carries its meaning from the Information Technology Act 2000
(rule 6(2)).

Policy Section 13 reproduces the list as the floor it is, and 13.2 states the point
that follows: meeting every item does not by itself make the safeguards reasonable;
failing any makes them unreasonable. Item (f) is why annex A6.2 puts the security
safeguards clause first among the contract terms, and 13.5 is why an AI tool that
cannot evidence access is not approved for personal data.

### 3.8 Transfers: a specific-order regime, not an adequacy regime

**Rule 15** - personal data processed by a Data Fiduciary under the Act "may be
transferred outside the territory of India subject to the restriction that the Data
Fiduciary shall meet such requirements as the Central Government may, by general or
special order, specify in respect of making such personal data available to any foreign
State, or to any person or entity under the control of or any agency of such a State."

Two features:

- **Transfer is permitted by default**, subject to requirements the Central Government
  may specify. There is no adequacy list, no standard clauses, and no prior
  authorisation in the held text.
- **The restriction is directed at making data available to a foreign State**, or to a
  person or entity under its control or agency - not at commercial transfers as such.

**No general or special order is held.** Policy 17.2 says so and routes the position to
the data protection lead.

Separately, **rule 13(4)** imposes an outright localisation duty on Significant Data
Fiduciaries for personal data the Central Government specifies, and for the traffic
data of its flow. No specification is held.

### 3.9 Rights machinery and the ninety-day grievance period

**Rule 14(1)** - the Data Fiduciary and, where applicable, the Consent Manager, shall
prominently publish on its website or app (a) the details of the means by which a Data
Principal may make a request to exercise her rights, and (b) the particulars, such as a
username or other identifier, which may be required to identify her under its terms of
service.

**Rule 14(2)** - she may make the request to the Data Fiduciary to whom she has
previously given consent, using the means and particulars required.

**Rule 14(3)** - every Data Fiduciary and Consent Manager shall prominently publish
its grievance redressal system for responding to grievances **within a reasonable
period not exceeding ninety days**, and shall implement appropriate technical and
organisational measures to ensure the system responds within that period.

**Rule 14(4)** - she may **nominate** one or more individuals to exercise her rights,
in accordance with the terms of service and applicable law.

**Rule 14(5)** - "identifier" means any sequence of characters issued by the Data
Fiduciary to identify her, including a customer identification file number, customer
acquisition form number, application reference number, enrolment ID, email address,
mobile number or licence number.

**The rights themselves are conferred by the Act and are not stated in this set.**
Rule 14 is machinery for exercising them. Policy 16.6 says so.

The drafting of rule 14(3) runs the publication obligation and the ninety-day response
period together. This set reads the ninety days as the outer limit for responding, and
policy 16.2 treats it as an outer limit rather than a target while requiring an
internal service level.

### 3.10 The Consent Manager - an institution with no direct analogue

**Rule 4** - a person fulfilling the conditions in **First Schedule Part A** may apply
to the Board for registration as a Consent Manager. The Board may inquire, register and
publish particulars, or reject with reasons; may direct remedial measures for
non-adherence after a hearing; may suspend or cancel registration and give directions
in the interests of Data Principals, after a hearing and for recorded reasons; and may
require information.

**Part A conditions** include: incorporation as a company in India; sufficient
technical, operational and financial capacity; sound financial condition and general
character of management; **net worth not less than two crore rupees**; adequate volume
of business, capital structure and earning prospects; fit and proper directors, key
managerial personnel and senior management; constitutional documents containing
required provisions; operations in the interests of Data Principals; and independent
certification.

**Part B obligations** include, among others: enabling consent to be given directly to
an onboarded Data Fiduciary or routed through another; **ensuring that the manner of
making available or sharing the personal data is such that the contents are not
readable by the Consent Manager**; maintaining a record of consents given, denied and
withdrawn, the notices preceding them, and the sharing of personal data, giving the
individual access to that record, making it available in machine-readable form on
request, and **retaining it for at least seven years**; maintaining a website or app as
the primary means of access; **not sub-contracting or assigning its obligations**;
taking reasonable security safeguards; **acting in a fiduciary capacity** in relation
to the Data Principal; avoiding conflicts of interest with Data Fiduciaries including
their promoters and key managerial personnel; publishing ownership and management
information including holders of more than two per cent of shares; maintaining audit
mechanisms; and not transferring control of the registered company without approval.

Policy 2.1 introduces the role and 7.5 states the consequence: routing consent through
a Consent Manager does not move responsibility for the notice or for the lawfulness of
what [ORGANISATION] then does. Rule 4 commences on **13 November 2026**, ahead of the
rest.

### 3.11 The corrigenda changed nothing

**G.S.R. 892(E) of 10 December 2025** makes five numbered corrections to G.S.R. 846(E),
comprising eight changes, all typographical:

| Location | For | Read |
|---|---|---|
| p.24 line 22 | "of this Gazette" | "in the Official Gazette" |
| p.24 line 24 | "of this Gazette" | "in the Official Gazette" |
| p.29 line 44 | "Department" | "Departments" |
| p.32 line 4 | "given in such" | "given in such order" |

| p.38 lines 1-15 | "(a) to (f)" | "(a) to (g)" |

**No obligation, date, threshold or period is altered.**

The two page-24 corrections are identifiable with certainty. The held text of rule 1
reads: "(2) Rules 1, 2 and 17 to 21 shall come into force on the date of their
publication **in the Official Gazette**. (3) Rule 4 shall come into force one year
after the date of publication **of this Gazette**. (4) Rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23
shall come into force eighteen months after the date of publication **of this
Gazette**." Sub-rule (2) was already in the corrected form; sub-rules (3) and (4) were
not. The corrigendum changes exactly that phrase at the two lines where it occurs.
**It is a correction of expression, and the commencement dates are unchanged.**

The version held by [ORGANISATION] is the original, uncorrected text; the corrigendum
is held separately and read against it.

Recording this matters for a reason beyond the content: the project had been carrying
this corrigendum as an unclosed gap. It is now held, and the answer is that it changes
nothing. Annex A3.6 nonetheless keeps version tracking as a standing item, because a
text that has moved once can move again.

### 3.12 The Board and the Appellate Tribunal

**G.S.R. 845(E)** - under section 19(1) of the Act, the Board **shall consist of four
members**.

**Rule 17** - Search-cum-Selection Committees. For the Chairperson: the Cabinet
Secretary as chairperson, the Secretaries in charge of the Department of Legal Affairs
and of MeitY, and two experts of repute. For other Members: the Secretary of MeitY as
chairperson, the Secretary of the Department of Legal Affairs, and two experts. The
Central Government appoints after considering suitability. A vacancy, absence or defect
in constitution does not invalidate proceedings.

**Rule 19** - meeting procedure: the Chairperson fixes date, time, place and agenda;
meetings chaired by the Chairperson or, in her absence, a Member chosen by those
present; **quorum of one-third**; decisions by majority with a casting vote; a Member
with an interest does not participate or vote; emergency action by the Chairperson with
recorded reasons, communicated within seven days and laid for ratification; decisions
by circulation on the Chairperson's direction; authentication of orders. **Rule 19(9) -
an inquiry shall be completed within six months of receipt of the intimation,
complaint, reference or direction under section 27 of the Act, extendable for recorded
reasons by not more than three months at a time.**

**Rule 20** - the Board functions as a **digital office** and may adopt techno-legal
measures so that proceedings do not require physical presence, without prejudice to its
power to summon and examine on oath.

**Rule 22** - appeal to the Appellate Tribunal, **filed in digital form**; fee equal to
that for an appeal under the TRAI Act 1997 unless reduced or waived by the Tribunal's
Chairperson, payable digitally via UPI or another RBI-authorised system; the Tribunal is
not bound by the Code of Civil Procedure but is guided by natural justice, may regulate
its own procedure, and **also functions as a digital office**.

Annex A11.3 and A11.4 rest on these. The practical point is that proceedings are
digital and time-boxed, so the ability to produce records quickly is itself a control.

### 3.13 The Act has an accuracy duty aimed at decisions, and it is not a house standard

s.8(3) requires a Data Fiduciary to ensure the completeness, accuracy and consistency
of personal data that is likely to be used to make a decision that affects the Data
Principal, or to be disclosed to another Data Fiduciary. Read with s.12(2), which
obliges correction, completion and updating on request, this is the closest thing the
Indian framework has to a rule about automated decision-making: not a right to
explanation or a right to human review, but a duty about the data the decision rests
on. v1.0 of this set could not see it and called the whole of policy Section 10 a house
standard. Policy 7.9 and 10.6 now carry it as law. The correction is recorded at 1.2.

### 3.14 Children: two prohibitions that consent cannot cure

s.9(2) prohibits processing likely to cause a detrimental effect on a child's
well-being; s.9(3) prohibits tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising
directed at children. Neither is conditioned on consent, and the Fourth Schedule
exemption reaches s.9(1) and s.9(3) but not s.9(2). For AI this is the provision that
bites on recommendation and engagement systems: they are behavioural monitoring by
design. Policy 9.6.

### 3.15 The employment ground, and where it stops

s.7(i) permits processing for the purposes of employment or for safeguarding the
employer from loss or liability, with a list of examples. Two boundaries are in the
words. First, "employee": the ground is framed around a Data Principal who is an
employee, and this set does not extend it to candidates. Second, "safeguarding": the
ground does not license monitoring beyond what protecting the employer requires.
Policy 7.6 states both limits; where they are exceeded, consent is the only route.

### 3.16 Foreign clients' data

s.17(1)(d) removes most of Chapter II, all of Chapter III and s.16 from processing, by
a person based in India under a contract with a person outside India, of the personal
data of Data Principals not within India. What survives is s.8(1) (responsibility for
processing) and s.8(5) (security safeguards). For an Indian processor serving foreign
clients this is the most consequential single provision in the Act, and it is in the
exemptions chapter. Policy 2.2 states it and requires a per-client record.

### 3.17 Penalties are a Schedule, and s.43A goes

The Schedule to s.33 sets seven ceilings, the largest (250 crore rupees) for failing to
take reasonable security safeguards, not for a breach as such. s.33(2)(e) makes
mitigation and its timeliness a factor, which is why policy 22.2 ties the breach
procedure to it. s.44(2)(a) omits s.43A of the Information Technology Act, 2000 (the
compensation provision for negligent handling of sensitive personal data) with effect
from 13 May 2027: the old regime is replaced, not layered.

## 5. Provisions held but not used

**Rule 5 and the Second Schedule** - standards for processing by the State and its
instrumentalities for the provision or issue of a subsidy, benefit, service,
certificate, licence or permit under section 7(b) of the Act, and for the purposes in
section 17(2)(b). Rule 5(2) construes "under law", "under policy" and "using public
funds". Not used because [ORGANISATION] is assumed not to be the State or an
instrumentality of it. **If it is, or acts for one, this set does not cover that
processing** and the Second Schedule standards apply.

**Rule 16** - the provisions of the Act do not apply to processing necessary for
research, archiving or statistical purposes if carried on in accordance with the Second
Schedule standards. Read against s.17(2)(b) of the Act, the exemption is conditioned
on the personal data **not being used to take any decision specific to a Data
Principal**. Not used in the policy: model development on personal data under this
exemption is possible in principle, but a model that will later inform decisions about
the same individuals sits uneasily with the condition, and this set does not take a
position on where that line falls. **Any organisation that wants to develop models
under the research exemption should have counsel read s.17(2)(b) against the Second
Schedule before relying on it.**

**Rules 17 to 21, Fifth and Sixth Schedules** - Board constitution, salaries and terms
of service. Used only for the commencement position and annex A11.3.

## 9. Method

Every provision cited above was read in the gazetted English text of the Act (No. 22 of
2023) or of G.S.R. 843(E), 844(E), 845(E), 846(E) or 892(E). The Act was obtained as the
Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology's copy of the Gazette print; the
authentic version is the eGazette publication, CG-DL-E-12082023-248045. The Hindi text was used only to confirm the
document's identity. Nothing in this set was taken from a web page, a summary, a
law-firm note, or a comparative overview. Where a requirement is left to an order,
specification or notification that is not held, the set names the gap instead of
filling it from any other source.
