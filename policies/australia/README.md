# Australia - AI Acceptable Use Policy set

**Status: PUBLISHED - verified within its stated scope: the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth)
as compiled on 4 June 2026 and the Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024,
read with the Information Commissioner's AI guidance and the government's AI adoption
guidance.** Every citation was read in the held instruments; the state and territory
laws, consumer law, anti-discrimination law and sector standards that also reach AI are
named as not held, and the set says so rather than pretending to be complete.
**Educational, not legal advice** - review by qualified counsel before adoption.

## Purpose

An acceptable use policy for AI tools in an organisation subject to Australian law,
with an enterprise governance annex. Obligations are stated as rules a team can apply;
provision identifiers live in the research note, not the policy body.

## What shapes the Australian edition

1. **There is no AI Act, and the government has said there will not be one.** The
   National AI Plan (December 2025) chose existing law as the foundation and dropped the
   mandatory-guardrails proposal. What organisations are asked to do is the National AI
   Centre's six essential practices. They are not law; the set adopts them as house
   standards and labels them.
2. **The Privacy Act reaches AI inputs, outputs and inferences without mentioning AI.**
   Personal information is information about an identifiable person whether true or not,
   so a hallucination about a customer is personal information. Entering it is a use or a
   disclosure; generating or inferring it is a collection. The regulator has said so, and
   the set treats that as the operating position.
3. **An automated-decision transparency duty commences on 10 December 2026, and the
   compiled Act does not show it.** The provisions are uncommenced and live only in the
   amending Act and the endnotes. The duty attaches to decisions made after commencement
   whatever the age of the system or the data, so the set makes the determination and
   drafts the disclosure from today. The duty is disclosure only - no right to contest,
   explain or be notified - and the set supplies those as house standards.
4. **A statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy has applied since 10 June 2025.**
   Actionable without damage, for intentional or reckless intrusion or misuse of
   information. AI-enabled monitoring, scraping, profiling and biometric identification
   are the uses that create the exposure, and the set treats them accordingly.

## The commencement finding

The automated-decision provisions were enacted in December 2024 to commence twenty-four
months after Royal Assent: **10 December 2026**. The compilation of 4 June 2026 does not
show them, because uncommenced amendments are not shown in compiled text. The set read
them in the amending Act and confirmed them against the regulator's own reproduction. The
statutory tort commenced on **10 June 2025**; the express statement that security steps
include technical and organisational measures on **11 December 2024**. The set states
live duties as live and dated duties with their dates - verified against the amending
Act's commencement table and the compilation's endnotes, not the reporting.

## Open gates (stated, not hidden)

1. **The regulator's final transparency guidance is not held** - expected before
   commencement; the working reading of "substantially and directly related" rests on the
   statute and the Explanatory Memorandum passages the regulator has quoted.
2. **The Children's Online Privacy Code is not held** - to be registered by 10 December
   2026; the set names the obligation and does not anticipate the content.
3. **Other laws that reach AI are not held** - state and territory privacy and
   surveillance-device laws, the Australian Consumer Law, anti-discrimination law and the
   Fair Work Act, the Online Safety Act, prudential and critical-infrastructure standards.
   Each is named where it bears on a clause; none is stated.
4. **The set cannot be run through the Data Privacy platform's verification pipeline**
   because Australia is not one of the platform's seven jurisdictions. Verification is by
   direct reading, recorded in the source register.
