## 2. Findings

### 2.1 The compiled Act does not show the automated-decision duty

Compilation No. 104 shows the law as in force on 4 June 2026. Uncommenced amendments
are not shown in the text; they are recorded in the endnotes. Endnote 4 lists, for the
Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024, "sch 1 (items 87-89): 10 Dec 2026 (s
2(1) item 7)". Items 87 to 89 are Part 15, Automated decisions and privacy policies.
So APP 1 in the compiled text ends at 1.6, and a reader who holds only the compilation
would not find APP 1.7 to 1.9 at all. The text was read in the amending Act (Schedule
1, Part 15, item 88) and cross-checked against Attachment A of the OAIC issues paper,
which reproduces it. The two agree word for word.

### 2.2 The duty attaches to decisions, not to systems, and reaches back

Item 89 of Schedule 1 to the amending Act provides that the amendment applies in
relation to decisions made after commencement, whether the arrangement for the program
was made, the use of personal information occurred, or the personal information was
acquired or created before or after that commencement. Policy clause 1.3 and clause 4.4
state this. It is the reason the set treats the duty as a design requirement from today.

### 2.3 The duty is disclosure only

APP 1.7 requires the privacy policy to contain the APP 1.8 information. Nothing in Part
15 confers a right to an explanation, to human review, to contest the decision, or to be
notified when a decision is made. The OAIC issues paper says so in terms, contrasting
Western Australia's regime which does confer such rights (issues paper p. 10). Policy
clause 13.3 records the gap and clause 14 fills it as a house standard.

### 2.4 False information about a person is personal information

Section 6(1) defines personal information as information or an opinion about an
identified or reasonably identifiable individual, "whether the information or opinion
is true or not". The OAIC's commercial AI guidance applies this to hallucinations and
deepfakes expressly. Policy clause 1.2 and clause 7.3 rest on the definition, and the
guidance is cited as the regulator's confirmation, not as the source of the rule.

### 2.5 Generating or inferring personal information is a collection

The OAIC's commercial AI guidance states that where an entity uses AI to infer or
generate personal information, the Act treats this as a collection and APP 3 applies,
including the reasonably-necessary test (APP 3.2), lawful and fair means (APP 3.5) and
collection from the individual unless unreasonable or impracticable (APP 3.6(b)). This
is the regulator's reading of the Act's broad concept of collection; the policy adopts
it as the operating position (clause 7.2). It is guidance, and the register records it
at that tier.

### 2.6 There is no AI Act, and the government has said so

The National AI Plan (December 2025), "Keep Australians Safe", p. 27: "The government's
regulatory approach to AI will continue to build on Australia's robust existing legal and
regulatory frameworks, ensuring that established laws remain the foundation for
addressing and mitigating AI-related risks." Action 8 (p. 32) points organisations to
the Guidance for AI Adoption's six essential practices. The Voluntary AI Safety Standard
of September 2024 was superseded by that guidance on 21 October 2025 (the OAIC guidance,
written in 2024, still refers to the Standard's ten guardrails; the set follows the
current guidance). Policy clause 1.1 and clause 17.

### 2.7 The statutory tort commenced on 10 June 2025

Section 2 of the amending Act, table item 8: Schedule 2 commences on a day fixed by
Proclamation or, failing that, the day after six months from Royal Assent; column 3
records 10 June 2025. Endnote 4 of the compilation agrees. The compiled Act now carries
Schedule 2, Statutory Tort for Serious Invasions of Privacy; clause 7 states the cause
of action. Policy clause 1.4 and clause 18.

### 2.8 Technical and organisational measures are now in the security principle

APP 11.3, inserted by Schedule 1 Part 5 of the amending Act (commenced 11 December 2024
under table item 2), provides that the reasonable steps in APP 11.1 and 11.2 include
technical and organisational measures. Policy clause 9.1.

### 2.9 Penalty tiers

Section 13G(1): serious interference with privacy; s 13G(3) sets the body corporate
maximum at the greater of $50,000,000, three times the benefit, or 30 per cent of
adjusted turnover during the breach turnover period. Section 13H: interference with
privacy, maximum 2,000 penalty units. Section 13K: infringement and compliance notices
for breaches of listed APPs, to which item 87 of the amending Act adds APP 1.7 from 10
December 2026. Policy clause 4.1 states the structure without figures beyond the
statutory maximum for s 13G, which the Act itself states in dollars.

### 2.10 The employee records exemption and the decision not to rely on it

Section 7B(3) exempts an act or practice of an employer directly related to a current
or former employment relationship and an employee record. The set does not rely on it
(policy clause 2.1.3). This is a house decision: the exemption is narrow, does not reach
the tort, and the OAIC's recruitment example treats AI in hiring as within the Act's
concerns. No provision of the Act is asserted to apply where the exemption applies; the
policy applies its own standard.

### 2.11 The regulator's transparency guidance is not final

The OAIC issues paper (May 2026) states the OAIC intends to release guidance by
September 2026, before commencement. It was not available at the time of writing. The
set relies on the statutory text and on the Explanatory Memorandum passages the issues
paper quotes (paras 336, 337, 343). The final guidance is an open verification (annex
A3, source register).

## 6. Points at which the set stops

- Where a matter turns on a registered APP code, the Privacy Regulation, or a prescribed
  country or binding scheme under APP 8.3, the set says the mechanism exists and does
  not say what it contains.
- The set states no penalty figure except the s 13G(3) maximum, which the Act states in
  dollars; penalty units are named without conversion.
- The set does not state what "significantly affect" or "substantially and directly
  related" mean beyond the statutory text and the Explanatory Memorandum passages the
  regulator has quoted. The regulator's final guidance may narrow or widen the working
  reading at clause 12.3; annex A3 holds the review.
- The Australian Consumer Law, anti-discrimination law, the Fair Work Act, the Online
  Safety Act, surveillance-device laws and sector standards are named because the
  implementation guidance names them as laws that may apply to AI harms. None is held.
  Nothing is asserted about their content.
