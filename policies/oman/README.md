# Sultanate of Oman - AI Acceptable Use Policy set

**Status: PUBLISHED - written from the enacted Arabic texts and checked against
MTCIT's official English texts.** Omani law is enacted in Arabic and the Arabic
governs; MTCIT's own English renderings of the Law, the Executive Regulations and
the national AI policy are held and were used as a checking instrument, never as
a substitute source. Every substantive figure, period and trigger in the set was
confirmed against them; the divergences found are recorded in the source
register.
**Educational, not legal advice** - review by qualified counsel before adoption.

## Purpose

An acceptable use policy for AI tools in an organisation subject to Omani law,
with an enterprise governance annex, built on the Personal Data Protection Law
(Royal Decree 6/2022), its Executive Regulations (Ministerial Decision 34/2024),
and the national policy for the safe and ethical use of AI systems (MTCIT,
April 2025). Obligations are stated as rules a team can apply; provision
identifiers live in the research note, not the policy body.

Focus 5 of the series (implementing and operating).

## What shapes the Oman edition

1. **There is a national AI policy, and it binds by its own terms.** Not a
   strategy document: it states controls that using and developing organisations
   commit to - ten for use, thirteen for development - and it reaches the
   private sector. Enforcement runs through each institution's own sector
   regulator, which may add AI provisions of its own; obtaining those issuances
   is the set's standing action.
2. **Two breach clocks, both 72 hours, with different triggers.** Notification
   to the regulator and notification to the individual run on the same period
   from the same starting point but on different tests - a breach can cross one
   threshold and not the other. The set requires a recorded determination for
   each limb and audits the distinction specifically.
3. **Cross-border transfer carries the framework's dominant penalty.** The
   transfer contravention is fined at OMR 100,000 to 500,000; everything else in
   the statute tops out at OMR 20,000. The minimum transfer fine is five times
   the maximum fine for anything else, so processing location is a gating
   question at tool approval, not a diligence detail.
4. **The sensitive-category permit is a licence, and tooling changes amend it.**
   Processing the sensitive categories needs a Ministry permit with a decision
   timetable and a five-year term - and four of the application fields are facts
   about tooling, so changing an AI supplier, adding a sub-processor, or moving
   a workload between regions engages a fifteen-day amendment duty, with
   cancellation among the consequences of missing it.

## The language finding

The Arabic distribution list of the national AI policy reads as government-only;
its own scope section, its definitions, and the official English distribution
list all confirm it reaches the private sector. A reader working only from the
Arabic distribution list would have concluded no private-sector Oman AI policy
was needed at all. The set records the divergence rather than silently resolving
it - and notes that the private-sector reach runs through sector regulation,
which is also why the sector regulator is the enforcement channel.

## Open gates (stated, not hidden)

1. **The sector regulator's issuances are not held** - for a private-sector
   organisation the sector regulator both enforces the national AI policy and
   may add AI provisions to it. Obtaining them is the recorded standing action.
2. **MTCIT's forms and guidance are not held** - the permit application form,
   breach report form, transfer assessment form and rights guidelines would
   sharpen the corresponding sections considerably.
3. **Five instruments the AI policy names as related are not held** - including
   the National Records Law (52/2024), which may qualify the erasure right, and
   the 2025 national data governance framework.
4. **The Electronic Defence Centre's competences are not held** - expressly
   preserved by the Law and central to the government-sector position; nothing
   about them is asserted.
