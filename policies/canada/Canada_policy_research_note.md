## 2. Findings

### 2.1 Quebec's section 12.1 is the only enacted automated-decision duty in Canada

Law 25 s 110 replaces ss 12 to 14 of the private-sector Act. New s 12.1: a person
carrying on an enterprise who uses personal information to render a decision based
exclusively on an automated processing must inform the person concerned no later than
when it informs them of the decision; must on request inform them of the personal
information used, the reasons and the principal factors and parameters, and the right
to have the information corrected; and must give the person the opportunity to submit
observations to a member of the personnel in a position to review the decision. In
force 22 September 2023 (s 175, default date). Section 90.1(5), enacted by s 159,
makes failure to inform or to give the opportunity to submit observations a ground
for a monetary administrative penalty. Policy clauses 1.3, 12, 13.

### 2.2 The duty turns on "exclusively"

Section 12.1 reaches decisions "based exclusively on an automated processing". A
decision a person makes with AI support is outside it. The set makes that a recorded
two-limb determination (clause 12) and applies the s 12.1 rights to substantially
automated decisions as a house standard (clause 13.2), because the commissioners'
principles (Accountability, organisations using generative AI) expect an effective
challenge mechanism and human review for "any administrative or otherwise significant
decision", and because Bill C-36 would reach predictions and recommendations as well as
decisions.

### 2.3 Bill C-36 would reach further, and is not law

Bill C-36 s 2 defines "automated decision system" as any technology that assists or
replaces the judgment of human decision-makers; s 62(1)(c) requires a general account
of the organisation's use of any automated decision system to make predictions,
recommendations or decisions that could have a legal or similarly significant effect;
s 63(4) gives a right on request to an explanation of such a prediction, recommendation
or decision; s 63(5) requires the explanation to indicate the type and source of
personal information used and the reasons or principal factors; s 63(6) requires an
opportunity to make written representations to an employee able to review; s 12(2)
lists appropriate-purpose factors including necessity, effectiveness, less intrusive
means and proportionality. The Summary states the bill repeals Part 1 of PIPEDA. First
reading 15 June 2026; no further stage. Policy clause 4.2; annex A3.

### 2.4 Inference is collection; public is not free

Commissioners' principles, Legal Authority and Consent: "the inference of information
about an identifiable individual (such as outputs about a person from a generative AI
system) will be considered a collection of personal information, and as such would
require legal authority." Limiting Collection: "the public accessibility of data does
not mean that it can be indiscriminately collected or used." PIPEDA s 7(1)(d) permits
collection without knowledge or consent of information that "is publicly available and
is specified by the regulations" - the regulations are not held, so the set says only
that the exception is confined to what they specify. Policy clauses 1.4, 7.2, 7.5.

### 2.5 Two breach regimes with different thresholds and different clocks

PIPEDA s 10.1(1): report to the Commissioner any breach of security safeguards where it
is reasonable to believe the breach creates a real risk of significant harm; s 10.1(2)
as soon as feasible after determining the breach has occurred; s 10.1(3) notify the
individual on the same threshold; s 10.1(5)-(6) conspicuous, direct, as soon as
feasible; s 10.1(7) significant harm defined; s 10.1(8) factors; s 10.3 records of every
breach, provided to the Commissioner on request; s 28 knowing contravention of s 10.1
or s 10.3(1) is an offence with fines up to $100,000. Law 25 s 103 enacts s 3.5:
reasonable measures to reduce injury and prevent recurrence; where the incident
presents a risk of serious injury, promptly notify the Commission and the persons
concerned; s 3.6 defines confidentiality incident; s 3.7 the risk factors and
consultation of the person in charge; s 3.8 the register, copied to the Commission on
request. In force 22 September 2022 (s 175(2)). Policy clause 20.5; annex A9.

### 2.6 Quebec's transfer assessment reaches cloud AI

Law 25 s 111 replaces s 17: before communicating personal information outside Quebec,
a privacy impact assessment of sensitivity, purposes, protection measures including
contractual ones, and the receiving state's legal framework; communication only if the
assessment establishes adequate protection, under a written agreement reflecting it.
The third paragraph extends this to entrusting a person outside Quebec with collecting,
using, communicating or keeping the information on the enterprise's behalf. Policy
clause 9.4; annex A6.

### 2.7 Profiling functions must be disclosed and are opt-in by design

Law 25 s 107 enacts s 8.1: where personal information is collected using technology
with functions allowing the person to be identified, located or profiled, the person is
informed first of the use of the technology and of the means available to activate the
functions. The definition of profiling names work performance, economic situation,
health, personal preferences, interests and behaviour. Policy clause 8.4.

### 2.8 The commencement schedule

Law 25 s 175: default date 22 September 2023; the listed exceptions include s 103
insofar as it enacts ss 3.1 and 3.5 to 3.8 (22 September 2022), and s 120 insofar as it
enacts the third paragraph of s 27 (portability, 22 September 2024). Policy clause 4.1.

### 2.9 Penalties

Law 25 s 159 enacts s 90.1, the monetary administrative penalty grounds; the maximum for a person other
than a natural person is $10,000,000 or, if greater, 2% of worldwide turnover for the
preceding fiscal year; s 160 replaces ss 91 to 92.1: penal proceedings carry fines up to $25,000,000 or, if greater,
4% of worldwide turnover. PIPEDA s 28: offences for knowing contravention of the breach
provisions, up to $100,000. Policy clause 1.2 states the Quebec administrative maximum;
the set otherwise names the regimes without figures.

## 6. Points at which the set stops

- The regulations under both Acts (breach records, incident notice content, publicly
  available information) are not held; the set says a regulation governs and does not
  say what it contains.
- The consolidated Quebec Act is not held; provisions are stated as enacted by Law 25.
  Any amendment since 2021 to the provisions relied on would not be reflected.
- Alberta's and British Columbia's Acts are named as substantially similar provincial
  legislation under PIPEDA s 26(2)(b) and not stated.
- No Quebec access-request timing is stated, because the relevant section was not
  amended by Law 25 and so is not in the held text.
- Bill C-36 is described as introduced; nothing in it is stated as a duty.
