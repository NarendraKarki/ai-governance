**Effective date:** [DATE]  **Version:** 1.0
**Policy owner:** [CISO]  **Privacy lead:** [Person in charge of the protection of personal information / Chief Privacy Officer]
**Classification:** Internal

## 1. Purpose

[ORGANISATION] permits the use of artificial intelligence (AI) tools to improve
productivity, provided that use protects the organisation's data, its customers and
staff, its intellectual property, and its obligations under Canadian law. This policy
sets the requirements for all such use.

Four features of the Canadian framework shape this document.

### 1.1 There is no Canadian AI Act, and the federal privacy reform is a bill, not law

The federal artificial intelligence bill died with its parent bill when Parliament was
prorogued in January 2025. A third attempt at private-sector privacy reform, Bill C-36,
the Protecting Privacy and Consumer Data Act, had first reading on 15 June 2026 and is
not law. **This policy relies on nothing in it.** Clause 4 says what it would add if
enacted, so that nothing has to be discovered on the day; every duty stated as binding
below is in an Act that is in force.

### 1.2 Two privacy regimes apply, and the stricter one is provincial

Federally, the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act governs
personal information collected, used or disclosed in the course of commercial
activities, and employee information only within federal works, undertakings and
businesses. In Quebec, the Act respecting the protection of personal information in
the private sector, as rewritten by Law 25 between 2022 and 2024, governs every
enterprise carrying on business there, employees included, with a privacy impact
assessment duty for new systems, a mandatory officer, an express-consent rule for
sensitive information, a transfer assessment before information leaves Quebec, and
administrative penalties up to the greater of ten million dollars or two per cent of
worldwide turnover. Clause 3 routes each obligation; where the two differ, this policy
applies the stricter rule everywhere, as a house standard, so that one policy can be
followed by one workforce.

### 1.3 The only enacted automated-decision duty in Canada is Quebec's

Since 22 September 2023, an enterprise that uses personal information to render a
decision **based exclusively on automated processing** must tell the person so, no
later than when it tells them the decision; must on request tell them the personal
information used, the reasons and the principal factors and parameters, and their
right to have that information corrected; and must give them the opportunity to make
observations to a member of staff able to review the decision. Failing to inform or
to give that opportunity is an administrative penalty ground. The duty turns on the
word "exclusively": a decision a person meaningfully makes with AI support is
outside it. Clause 12 makes that a recorded determination for every system, and
clause 13 applies the same rights to substantially automated decisions as a house
standard, because the line between the two is exactly where a regulator will look.

### 1.4 Inferring is collecting, and public does not mean free

Canada's privacy commissioners have stated that an inference about an identifiable
individual, including a generative AI output about a person, is a collection of
personal information and needs legal authority; and that personal information
accessible online remains subject to Canadian law even where it is described as
publicly available. The federal Act permits collection of publicly available
information without consent only where regulations specify it. Clause 7 rests on both.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, temporary staff, and third parties
who use [ORGANISATION] systems, data, or accounts. It covers all AI tools, including
public and generative AI services, AI features embedded in other software, coding
assistants, meeting and note-taking assistants, media generators, AI agents, and
internal AI systems. It applies on company devices and accounts, and on personal
devices or accounts whenever used for [ORGANISATION] work or data.

Compliance is mandatory. Breaches may result in disciplinary action up to and
including termination.

### 2.1 Which law reaches [ORGANISATION]

2.1.1 **The federal Act** applies to personal information [ORGANISATION] collects,
uses or discloses in the course of commercial activities, and to employee and
applicant information where [ORGANISATION] is a federal work, undertaking or
business. [ORGANISATION] is [a federally regulated / not a federally regulated]
employer, and the data protection lead has recorded the determination.

2.1.2 **The Quebec Act** applies to [ORGANISATION] as an enterprise carrying on
business in Quebec, in respect of personal information it collects, holds, uses or
communicates, including about employees and applicants.

2.1.3 **Where a provincial private-sector Act has been declared substantially similar**
to the federal Act, that Act applies within the province in place of the federal Act
for intra-provincial activity. Alberta's and British Columbia's Acts are not held and
are not stated in this policy (clause 22).

2.1.4 **Employees outside Quebec.** Where [ORGANISATION] is not a federal work, the
federal Act does not reach its employee information outside Quebec. **This policy
applies to all employee and applicant information anyway, as a house standard**,
because the Quebec Act reaches it for Quebec staff, because the commissioners'
principles treat employment as a high-impact context for AI, and because one rule for
one workforce is the only rule that will be followed.

2.1.5 Health-sector privacy laws, provincial consumer protection and human rights
laws, and the federal government's own directives on automated decision-making
(which bind federal institutions, not [ORGANISATION]) are named at clause 22 and not
stated.

## 3. Which law reaches which use

3.1 **Appropriate purposes reach everything.** Under the federal Act, personal
information may be collected, used or disclosed only for purposes a reasonable
person would consider appropriate in the circumstances, whatever consent has been
obtained. Under the Quebec Act, collection requires a serious and legitimate reason
and purposes determined before collection. Every AI use of personal information is
tested against both before consent is even considered.

3.2 **Consent.** Federally, knowledge and consent are required for collection, use
and disclosure except where an exception applies, and consent is valid only where a
person to whom the activity is directed would understand the nature, purpose and
consequences of what they are consenting to. In Quebec, consent must be clear, free
and informed, and **express** where sensitive information is concerned. This policy
applies the Quebec standard everywhere.

3.3 **Secondary use.** Federally, information may not be used or disclosed for a
purpose other than the one it was collected for except with consent or as required by
law, and a new purpose must be documented. In Quebec, use for another purpose without
consent is confined to a closed list: a purpose consistent with the original one
(direct and relevant connection; commercial prospection never qualifies), clear
benefit to the person, fraud prevention and security, delivering what the person
requested, and de-identified study, research or statistics. This policy applies the
Quebec list everywhere (clause 7.1).

3.4 **Privacy impact assessments.** The Quebec Act requires one for any project to
acquire, develop or overhaul an information system involving personal information,
with the person in charge consulted from the outset, and one before personal
information is communicated outside Quebec. The federal Act does not require one; the
commissioners' principles expect one. This policy requires one for every AI system
involving personal information (clause 7 and clause 9).

3.5 **Automated decisions.** Quebec's duty at clause 1.3 applies in Quebec. Nothing
equivalent is in force federally. This policy applies the Quebec rights everywhere,
and to substantially automated decisions as well as exclusively automated ones, as a
house standard (clause 13).

3.6 **Breaches.** Federally, a breach of security safeguards that creates a real risk
of significant harm must be reported to the Privacy Commissioner and notified to the
individual as soon as feasible, and every breach must be recorded. In Quebec, a
confidentiality incident presenting a risk of serious injury must be notified promptly
to the Commission d'accès à l'information and to the persons concerned, and every
incident registered. Clause 20.5 operates both.

## 4. What binds today, and what would change

4.1 **In force now.** The federal Act's ten principles and its appropriate-purposes,
valid-consent, access and breach provisions. The Quebec Act as amended: the person in
charge and the incident regime since 22 September 2022; the privacy impact assessment,
express consent, secondary-use list, automated-decision duty, profiling notice,
transfer assessment and penalty provisions since 22 September 2023; the right to
receive computerised personal information in a structured format since 22 September
2024.

4.2 **Pending, not relied on.** Bill C-36 would repeal Part 1 of the federal Act and
replace it. As introduced it defines an automated decision system as any technology
that assists or replaces the judgment of human decision-makers; requires an
organisation's public policies to give a general account of its use of such systems to
make predictions, recommendations or decisions that could have a legal or similarly
significant effect; gives individuals a right on request to an explanation of such a
prediction, recommendation or decision, stating the type and source of personal
information used and the reasons or principal factors, and an opportunity to make
written representations to an employee able to review it; and adds appropriate-purpose
factors including necessity, less intrusive means and proportionality. **None of that
is law.** Clause 13 already meets it as a house standard; if the bill is enacted, the
annex records what changes from house standard to duty.

4.3 **Guidance, not law.** The federal, provincial and territorial commissioners'
Principles for responsible, trustworthy and privacy-protective generative AI
technologies (December 2023) state the regulators' reading of existing law. The
document itself says that many of its "shoulds" are requirements of applicable
privacy law. This policy follows them as the operating interpretation and says where
it does.

## 5. Levels of use

Every use of an AI tool is classified at one of four levels before it begins. The
approved tools list states the highest level each tool may be used at. This
classification is [ORGANISATION]'s own and is not a legal category.

**Level 1 - General assistance.** Drafting, summarising, translating, brainstorming,
coding with no [ORGANISATION] data beyond the prompt. No personal information, no
confidential information.

**Level 2 - Internal information.** Use with [ORGANISATION] internal documents,
non-public business information, or personal information about staff or customers that
is not sensitive. Permitted only with tools approved for Level 2, whose terms prevent
the supplier using inputs and outputs for its own purposes.

**Level 3 - Sensitive information or high privacy risk.** Any use involving
sensitive personal information (medical, biometric or otherwise intimate by nature,
or sensitive by the context of its use); any use that generates or infers personal
information; any profiling; any customer-facing AI; any use in relation to minors.
Requires a privacy impact assessment, a named accountable person, and approval by the
person in charge of the protection of personal information.

**Level 4 - Decisions about people.** Any use where the AI system renders, or
substantially contributes to, a decision about an individual that could have a legal
or similarly significant effect: eligibility, pricing, credit, employment, access to a
service, enforcement of a contract. Everything at Level 3, plus the clause 12
determination, the clause 13 rights, the human oversight at clause 14, and approval by
the AI Governance Committee.

## 6. Permitted and prohibited use

6.1 **Permitted**, within the tool's approved level: drafting and editing; summarising
documents the user is entitled to read; research and analysis of non-personal data;
code generation and review subject to clause 10; transcription and minutes subject to
clause 7.6; customer service and content subject to clause 15; internal analytics
subject to clause 7.

6.2 **Prohibited in all circumstances:**

- entering personal information, sensitive information, confidential information,
  credentials, source code or customer data into a tool not approved for that level;
- prompting an AI tool to re-identify de-identified or anonymised information;
- profiling or categorising people in a way that may lead to unfair, unethical or
  discriminatory treatment, or collecting, using or disclosing personal information
  for purposes known or likely to cause significant harm - the "no-go zones" the
  commissioners have named;
- using AI to create deepfakes or intimate images of an identifiable person, to bypass
  an authentication system, or to generate and publish false or defamatory
  information about a person;
- using a conversational bot to nudge people into divulging personal information they
  would not otherwise give;
- rendering a Level 4 decision exclusively by automated processing (clause 14);
- scraping or acquiring personal information from the internet or third parties
  without the approval clause 7.5 requires;
- representing AI output as verified fact where clause 11 requires verification;
- disabling, circumventing or ignoring a control on an approved tool.

6.3 **Prohibited without written approval from the person in charge:** any use at
Level 3 or Level 4; any fine-tuning or training on [ORGANISATION] data (clause 16); any
use of an AI agent that can take actions in other systems (clause 10.6); any AI use
directed at minors (clause 19); any AI tool that identifies, locates or profiles
people (clause 8.4).

## 7. Personal information in AI tools

### 7.1 Entering personal information is a use or a communication

7.1.1 Personal information entered into an AI tool is used, and where it becomes
accessible to the supplier or anyone outside [ORGANISATION]'s control, it is disclosed
(federally) or communicated to a third person (in Quebec). Both require consent or a
recognised exception, and both must be for a purpose the information was collected
for or one the law permits.

7.1.2 Using AI as the means of carrying out the purpose the information was collected
for is within that purpose. Using the information for an AI purpose of its own
(training, profiling, enrichment) is a new purpose: it is documented, and it proceeds
only with consent or within the Quebec consistent-purpose list at clause 3.3.
Commercial prospection is never a consistent purpose.

7.1.3 The minimum personal information sufficient for the purpose is entered.
Anonymised, synthetic or de-identified information is used in prompts instead of
personal information wherever the purpose allows. Prompts containing personal
information are not retained, used for secondary purposes or disclosed unless
required, and the retention period is on the approved tools list.

7.1.4 A person's consent to a product or service is not conditioned on consent to AI
processing beyond what the explicitly specified and legitimate purposes require.

### 7.2 Generating or inferring personal information is a collection

7.2.1 An inference or output about an identifiable individual is personal
information, and producing it is a collection that requires legal authority: an
appropriate purpose, a serious and legitimate reason, and consent or a recognised
exception. Inferences are made only for specified and disclosed purposes, and only
where their accuracy can be reasonably assessed and validated.

7.2.2 Generated or inferred personal information that [ORGANISATION] had no authority
to collect is destroyed or anonymised as soon as practicable.

7.2.3 Information about a person that is false is still information about that
person. A hallucinated or fabricated statement about an identifiable individual is
handled as personal information: subject to the accuracy duty, to access and
correction, and to the prohibition on generating and publishing false information.

### 7.3 Sensitive information

7.3.1 Sensitive personal information is information that, by its nature (medical,
biometric or otherwise intimate) or by the context of its use or communication,
entails a high reasonable expectation of privacy. Federally, sensitivity determines
the form of consent, and express consent is generally required; in Quebec, express
consent is required. This policy requires express consent everywhere.

7.3.2 Using AI to generate, infer or classify sensitive information about a person is
a Level 3 use and requires express consent from that person first. In contexts where
consent may be inadequate, such as health, a separate review that considers privacy
and ethics, with independent oversight, precedes the use.

### 7.4 Employee and applicant information

AI use in recruitment, performance, monitoring and workforce decisions is a Level 3
use at least, and Level 4 where it bears on hiring, promotion, discipline or
termination. Employment is a context the commissioners have named as one where AI
discrimination is most likely, and clause 20.6 tests for it.

### 7.5 Scraping and third-party datasets

7.5.1 Personal information accessible online remains subject to Canadian privacy law.
The federal exception for publicly available information is confined to what the
regulations specify. Collecting personal information by scraping, or by acquiring a
third-party dataset, is a collection by [ORGANISATION], must have legal authority, and
proceeds only with a privacy impact assessment and written approval by the person in
charge.

7.5.2 Where personal information is sourced from a third party, [ORGANISATION]
establishes that the third party collected it lawfully and has authority to disclose
it, and records the basis.

### 7.6 Meeting recording and transcription

AI transcription collects whatever is said. It is used only with a tool approved for
Level 2 or above, only where participants are told at the outset that an AI tool is
recording and transcribing and can object, and only where the consequence of objecting
is not serious. Sensitive information captured that the meeting did not require is
deleted from the transcript.

## 8. Openness, notice and the privacy policy

8.1 [ORGANISATION] makes readily available, in clear and simple language, its
policies and practices for personal information in AI tools: the types of personal
information involved and a general account of their use; the identity and contact
details of the person accountable; how to gain access; what is made available to
related organisations; and whether personal information is communicated outside
Quebec or outside Canada.

8.2 Individuals are told what, how, when and why personal information is collected,
used or disclosed at each stage of an AI system's lifecycle for which [ORGANISATION]
is responsible, including where personal information collected from prompts is used
to train or refine a model, and what personal information was used to refine a model
for [ORGANISATION]'s use.

8.3 Where an AI tool will be used as part of a decision-making process, affected
people are told so, in what capacity, with what safeguards, and what recourse is
available, with a general description of how the system works and an overview of
potential outcomes.

8.4 **Identification, location and profiling.** Where personal information is
collected from a person using technology with functions that allow the person to be
identified, located or profiled, the person is told first of the use of that
technology and of the means available to activate those functions. Profiling means the
collection and use of personal information to assess characteristics of a person, in
particular work performance, economic situation, health, personal preferences,
interests or behaviour. Such functions are off by default and activated only by the
person.

## 9. Security, retention and transfers

9.1 Personal information in, or passing through, AI tools is protected by safeguards
appropriate to its sensitivity against loss, theft, and unauthorised access,
disclosure, copying, use or modification: physical, organisational and technological
measures, regardless of the format in which the information is held.

9.2 The AI-specific threats the commissioners have named are addressed for every tool:
prompt injection, model inversion and other extraction of training data, and
jailbreaking of privacy or security controls. Tools are tested for them before
approval (clause 20.6).

9.3 Personal information is retained only as long as necessary for the identified
purposes, and then destroyed, erased or anonymised. Information used to make a
decision about a person is retained long enough for the person to access it after the
decision. Retention schedules cover training data, prompts and outputs.

9.4 **Outside Quebec, outside Canada.** Before personal information is communicated
outside Quebec, including to an AI supplier or cloud service, a privacy impact
assessment considers the sensitivity, the purposes, the protection measures including
contractual ones, and the legal framework in the receiving state; the communication
proceeds only if the assessment establishes adequate protection, and under a written
agreement that reflects the assessment. Federally, [ORGANISATION] remains
responsible for information transferred to a third party for processing and uses
contractual or other means to provide a comparable level of protection. This policy
applies the Quebec assessment to every cross-border AI supplier.

## 10. Verification of output, code and agents

10.1 AI output is a draft. It is verified by a person with the competence to do so
before it is relied on, sent outside [ORGANISATION], or recorded as fact. The degree
of verification matches the consequence of error.

10.2 Legal, regulatory, financial, medical and safety content is verified against a
primary source. AI-cited authorities are checked to exist and to say what is claimed.

10.3 AI-generated code is reviewed as third-party code before it enters a repository.

10.4 Records show where information is the product of an AI output and identify the
data and system that generated it. Outputs that could have a significant impact on a
person or group are identified as AI-generated.

10.5 Where the outputs of a system are not meaningfully explainable, the use is
reconsidered; for Level 4 it is not approved.

10.6 **AI agents.** A tool that selects its own actions across other systems is a
Level 3 use at least and Level 4 where its actions affect a person. Every action it can
take is enumerated and approved; it runs with least privilege; its actions are logged;
a person can stop it.

## 11. Accuracy

11.1 Personal information is as accurate, complete and up to date as is necessary
for the purposes for which it is used, so that inappropriate information is not used
to make a decision about a person. AI carries heightened accuracy risk and the steps
taken match it.

11.2 Before approval, [ORGANISATION] evaluates the validity and reliability of the tool
for the intended purpose and across the circumstances of use, and the accuracy
limitations the supplier has disclosed: time-bounded or single-source training data,
inputs that tend to produce wrong output. Where these are not disclosed and cannot be
established, the use is reconsidered.

11.3 A person is responsible for verifying any personal information obtained through
AI and can overturn a result. Where a use relates to a specific group, that group is
adequately and accurately represented in the training data.

11.4 An individual may access personal information about them held in or generated by
AI tools, and challenge its accuracy and completeness. Federally, a request is answered
within thirty days, extendable once by thirty days with notice. Where inaccuracy is
demonstrated the information is corrected, deleted or added to, and third parties with
access are told; where a challenge is unresolved its substance is recorded.

## 12. Decisions about people: the determination

12.1 For every AI system that bears on a decision about an individual, the
accountable person records a written determination on two questions:

(a) is the decision **based exclusively on automated processing** of personal
information, so that the Quebec duty at clause 1.3 applies to it; and
(b) if not, does the system nonetheless **substantially contribute** to a decision
that could have a legal or similarly significant effect, so that the house standard
at clause 13 applies.

12.2 A decision is exclusively automated where no person exercises judgment on it
before it takes effect. A person who routinely approves what the system proposes,
without the information, time or authority to decide otherwise, is not exercising
judgment; such a decision is recorded as exclusively automated.

12.3 A decision is substantially contributed to where the system's output is a key
factor in the person's decision and directly connected to it: a ranking, a score, a
recommendation, an eligibility flag.

12.4 The determination is made on the use, not the tool, is recorded in the AI
register with its reasons, and is revisited on any material change.

12.5 The determination is not a permission. A system inside either limb is a Level 4
use.

## 13. Decisions about people: what the person is owed

13.1 For a decision based exclusively on automated processing, the Quebec Act
requires, and this policy requires everywhere:

(a) the person is informed that the decision was so rendered, no later than when they
are informed of the decision;
(b) on request, the person is told the personal information used to render the
decision, the reasons and the principal factors and parameters that led to it, and
their right to have the personal information used corrected; and
(c) the person is given the opportunity to submit observations to a member of staff
who is in a position to review the decision.

13.2 For a decision the system substantially contributes to, [ORGANISATION] provides
the same three things **as a house standard**. This is the right the commissioners'
principles describe (an effective challenge mechanism, sufficient information to
understand how the decision was reached, and human review or reconsideration on
request), and it is what the pending federal bill would require of predictions,
recommendations and decisions alike.

13.3 The explanation is given in plain language, matched to the seriousness of the
outcome, and is recorded. Where an explanation cannot be given because the system's
behaviour cannot be explained, the system is not used for that decision.

## 14. Human oversight of decisions about people

14.1 No Level 4 decision is rendered exclusively by automated processing. A person
with the authority and the competence to decide reviews the AI output, has access to
the information it rests on, and can reach a different decision. Approval and override
rates are monitored under clause 20.6, because a review that always agrees is not a
review, and would return the decision to the exclusively automated limb.

14.2 Accountability for the decision rests with [ORGANISATION], not with the system.

## 15. Customer-facing AI

15.1 Where a customer or member of the public interacts with an AI system, they are
told so at the start, told the privacy risks and the mitigations available to them
(including not entering personal information unless necessary), and can reach a
person.

15.2 Personal information collected through a customer-facing AI system is collected
only for appropriate purposes, only with valid consent or a recognised exception, and
never by nudging the person into disclosing what they would not otherwise disclose.

15.3 A customer-facing AI system is tested before launch for inaccurate, biased and
harmful outputs and for surfacing personal information about other people, and is
monitored after launch.

15.4 Where AI has generated or materially altered content presented as
[ORGANISATION]'s work, the use is disclosed where a reasonable person would want to
know and always where non-disclosure would mislead. Statements about what an AI
product does are accurate.

## 16. Training and fine-tuning on [ORGANISATION] data

16.1 Using personal information [ORGANISATION] holds to train or fine-tune a model,
or providing it to a supplier to do so, is a new purpose unless training was a purpose
identified at collection. It requires consent, or a Quebec consistent-purpose basis
(which will rarely be available: training a model has no direct and relevant connection
to serving the person), or de-identification before use for study, research or
statistical purposes.

16.2 De-identified information used for training is protected by reasonable measures
against re-identification, and no tool is prompted to re-identify it.

16.3 A supplier that fine-tunes on [ORGANISATION] data does so under a written
agreement that confines the data to [ORGANISATION]'s purposes, prohibits use to improve
the supplier's other models, requires filters so that personal information does not
surface in outputs, provides for deletion, and, where the supplier is outside Quebec,
reflects the clause 9.4 assessment.

16.4 Fine-tuning is a Level 3 use and requires a privacy impact assessment.

## 17. Necessity, proportionality and the no-go zones

17.1 Before an AI system is used where it may have a significant impact on people,
[ORGANISATION] establishes, on evidence, that the use is necessary and proportionate:
that the tool is more than potentially useful, that it is likely to be effective for the
specified purpose, and that no more privacy-protective means achieves the same end.

17.2 The no-go zones at clause 6.2 are absolute. If a use is found to be within one,
the activity ceases. Where a use has an unintended inappropriate application, the
supplier is told and the risk mitigated or the use withdrawn.

## 18. Accountability

18.1 [ORGANISATION] is responsible for personal information under its control,
including information transferred to a third party for processing. The person in
charge of the protection of personal information is [the person exercising the highest
authority], who has delegated the function in writing to [title]; the title and
contact information of the person in charge are published on [ORGANISATION]'s
website.

18.2 Policies and practices give effect to the privacy principles: procedures to
protect personal information, procedures to receive and respond to complaints and
inquiries, staff training, and information explaining the policies. This policy and its
annex are those policies for AI.

18.3 Privacy impact assessments and, for Level 4 systems, algorithmic impact
assessments are undertaken and revisited, including bias testing, as systems and
regulation evolve.

## 19. Minors and vulnerable people

19.1 Any AI use directed at minors, or in a service likely to be used by minors, is a
Level 3 use, requires written approval by the person in charge, and is designed in the
minor's interest. Minors are at particularly high risk of significant negative impact
from AI, are less able to identify or challenge biased or inaccurate information, and
need greater safeguards.

19.2 Every privacy impact assessment for an AI system identifies risks to vulnerable
groups, including groups that have historically experienced discrimination or bias,
and the measures taken to mitigate them.

## 20. Governance, records, incidents and monitoring

20.1 The AI Governance Committee, chaired by [the accountable executive], approves
Level 4 uses, owns this policy, and reviews the AI register quarterly.

20.2 The person in charge of the protection of personal information approves Level 3
uses, owns privacy impact assessments, is consulted from the outset of every project to
acquire, develop or overhaul a system involving personal information, holds the
clause 12 determinations, and is consulted on every incident risk assessment.

20.3 Every AI system has a named accountable person who owns its register entry, its
assessments and its monitoring.

20.4 **The AI register** records every AI system in use, including AI embedded in
other products: purpose, level, accountable person, the personal information it uses,
the legal authority for each collection, use and disclosure, where it processes data
and whether that is outside Quebec or Canada, the supplier's rights over inputs and
outputs, the clause 12 determination, the privacy impact assessment, the testing done,
the monitoring in place, and the retention periods for its data.

20.5 **Incidents.** Any suspected unauthorised access to, use, disclosure or loss of
personal information involving an AI tool is reported to [Security] immediately.
[ORGANISATION] takes reasonable measures to reduce the risk of injury and prevent
recurrence. The person in charge is consulted on the risk assessment, which considers
the sensitivity of the information, the anticipated consequences of its use, and the
likelihood it will be used for injurious purposes. Where the incident presents a risk
of serious injury, [ORGANISATION] promptly notifies the Commission d'accès à
l'information and the persons concerned. Where it is reasonable to believe the breach
creates a real risk of significant harm (bodily harm, humiliation, damage to
reputation or relationships, loss of employment or opportunities, financial loss,
identity theft, effects on credit, loss of property), [ORGANISATION] reports to the
Privacy Commissioner of Canada and notifies the individuals as soon as feasible after
determining the breach has occurred, conspicuously and directly. Every incident,
whether or not notified, is entered in the incident register, which is provided to a
commissioner on request. AI incidents that are not privacy incidents (harmful output,
a biased decision, an agent acting outside its approval) are also recorded and
investigated.

20.6 **Monitoring and testing.** Tools are tested before approval for the threats at
clause 9.2, and adversarially for unintended inappropriate uses. Level 4 systems are
tested before deployment and at least [annually] for differential outcomes on
protected grounds, and monitored for approval and override rates. Inappropriate uses
and biased outcomes not disclosed as a limitation are reported to the supplier.

20.7 **Complaints.** Individuals can complain about [ORGANISATION]'s handling of
their personal information in connection with AI through the mechanism the privacy
policy describes, and may complain to the Privacy Commissioner of Canada or the
Commission d'accès à l'information.

## 21. Training and acknowledgement

21.1 Staff who use AI tools with [ORGANISATION] data complete training covering this
policy, the levels of use, the prohibition on entering personal information into

21.3 Every person in scope acknowledges this policy in writing on joining and on each

## 22. What this policy does not cover
