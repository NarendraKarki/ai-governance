# Japan - AI Acceptable Use Policy set

**Status: PUBLISHED - verified within its stated scope: the Act on the Protection of
Personal Information (government English translation held) as the binding law, the AI
Promotion Act (Act No. 53 of 2025), and the AI Guidelines for Business v1.1.** Every
citation was read in the held instruments. The 2026 APPI amendment is named as pending
and relied on for nothing; the subordinate Cabinet Order and Enforcement Rules are
applied by reference.
**Educational, not legal advice** - review by qualified counsel before adoption.

## Purpose

An acceptable use policy for AI tools in an organisation subject to Japanese law, with
an enterprise governance annex. Obligations are stated as rules a team can apply;
provision identifiers live in the research note, not the policy body.

## What shapes the Japanese edition

1. **Japan's AI Act binds no one and penalises nothing.** The AI Promotion Act (Act No.
   53 of 2025) sets principles, a national basic plan and an AI Strategic Headquarters,
   and asks operators only to *endeavour* to use AI and cooperate with government. It
   creates no operator obligation, no individual right and no penalty. This is the
   defining feature: Japan's is a soft-law, innovation-first regime, and the set says so
   rather than dressing a promotion law as a compliance one.
2. **The binding law is the APPI, and it does not mention AI.** Every enforceable duty -
   purpose of use, no wrongful acquisition, consent for sensitive information, security,
   leak reporting, third-party and cross-border limits, disclosure, correction and
   cessation - is in the Act on the Protection of Personal Information and applies to AI
   handling unchanged. The set routes every AI use through it.
3. **Japan's individual lever against harmful AI processing is the cessation right.**
   The APPI gives no automated-decision right. Its instrument is Article 35(5): a person
   may demand that a business cease using, delete, or cease providing their data where
   the handling **is likely to harm their rights or interests** - the ground an AI use is
   most likely to engage. The set builds the individual-facing duty on it, and supplies
   automated-decision explanation and human review as house standards drawn from the AI
   Guidelines.
4. **The AI-specific expectations are voluntary, and a binding amendment is coming.** The
   AI Guidelines for Business (METI/MIC v1.1) set common guiding principles - human
   -centric, safety, fairness, privacy, security, transparency, accountability and more -
   adopted here as labelled house standards. The Personal Information Protection
   Commission has decided a 2026 APPI amendment (statistics/AI consent exception, under
   -16 rules, facial-feature-data duties, administrative monetary penalties), expected in
   the Diet in spring 2026; the set states it and relies on none of it.

## Open gates (stated, not hidden)

1. **The 2026 APPI amendment is not held as enacted text** - described from the
   Commission's reform policy; nothing in it is stated as a duty.
2. **The Cabinet Order and Enforcement Rules are applied by reference** - the exact
   leak-report timing, opt-out particulars and equivalent-measures standard sit there and
   are not restated as figures.
3. **The set turns on the government's English translations** - only the Japanese text
   has legal effect, and the set says so, staying with plain effect rather than
   translation wording.
4. **The set cannot be run through the Data Privacy platform's verification pipeline**
   because Japan is not one of the platform's seven jurisdictions. Verification is by
   direct reading, recorded in the source register.
