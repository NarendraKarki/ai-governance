# Research note - Japan AI policy set

**Built:** 3 September 2026
**Purpose:** every clause of the policy and annex traced to the provision or guidance it rests on. Provision identifiers live here, not in the policy body.
**Method:** primary sources only; every citation read in the held instrument; nothing asserted that is not held.

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 Japan's AI Act creates no obligation and no penalty

AI Promotion Act Article 7 makes a "utilization business operator" - anyone who develops
or provides AI products or services or uses AI in business - responsible only to
"endeavour to enhance the efficiency and advancement of business activities" through AI
and to "cooperate with the measures implemented by the national government". Article 8
puts the same endeavour on citizens. The binding actors are the national government
(Articles 4, 11-18), local governments (Article 5) and the AI Strategic Headquarters
under the Cabinet (Articles 19-28). There is no compliance duty on a private operator, no
individual right, and no penalty provision anywhere in the Act. Policy clause 1.1.

### 2.2 What the AI Act does reach: a transparency principle and a guidance channel

Article 3(4): where AI research or use is for wrong purposes or by inappropriate methods
that may facilitate crime, leak personal information, infringe copyright or otherwise
harm citizens' peaceful lives and their rights and interests, measures ensuring
appropriate implementation and transparency must be taken. Article 13 directs the
government to establish guidelines in accordance with international norms - the statutory
basis for the AI Guidelines for Business. Article 16 directs the government to analyse
cases where citizens' rights and interests are infringed by wrongful or inappropriate AI
use and to give guidance and advice to operators. The set treats Article 3(4) as a house
standard and Article 16 as the reason to keep the binding APPI compliance sound. Policy
clauses 1.1, 4.3, 17.

### 2.3 The binding duties are all in the APPI, and it does not mention AI

APPI Article 17: specify the purpose of use as far as possible; do not alter it beyond
what can be reasonably linked to the original. Article 18: do not handle personal
information beyond the scope necessary for the specified purpose without prior consent
(exceptions in 18(3)). Article 19: do not use personal information in a way that may
foment or induce an unlawful or unjust act. Article 20: no acquisition by deception or
wrongful means; no acquisition of sensitive personal information without prior consent
(exceptions in 20(2)). Article 21: notify or publicly announce the purpose of use on
acquisition; express indication at direct written acquisition. Articles 22-25: accuracy
and deletion when no longer needed; security measures; supervision of employees and
entrusted persons. Article 26: report leaks likely to harm rights and interests to the
Commission and notify the persons. Article 27: no third-party provision without consent
(exceptions; opt-out). Article 28: cross-border provision requires consent informed by
the foreign regime unless a recognised country or equivalent-measures system. Articles
33-35: disclosure, correction, cessation. None mentions AI; all apply to AI handling
unchanged. Policy clause 3, 7, 8, 9, 11.

### 2.4 Japan's individual lever against harmful AI processing is Article 35(5)

There is no automated-decision right in the APPI. Article 35(1) lets a person demand
cessation or deletion where data is handled in breach of Articles 18 or 19 or acquired in
breach of Article 20. Article 35(3) lets them demand cessation of third-party provision
made in breach of Article 27 or 28. Article 35(5) is the broad ground: a person may
demand cessation of use, deletion, or cessation of third-party provision where the data
is no longer needed, where a reportable leak has occurred, or **where handling the data
is likely to harm the identifiable person's rights or interests**. This is the provision
an AI use is most likely to engage, and the set builds the individual-facing duty on it
(policy clauses 12, 13.1). Article 35(6) requires the business to honour a well-founded
request without delay, subject to a disproportionate-difficulty exception with
alternative measures.

### 2.5 The AI-specific expectations are voluntary guidance

The AI Guidelines for Business (METI/MIC v1.1), issued under AI Promotion Act Article 13,
divide AI actors into developers, providers and business users, and set common guiding
principles: human-centric, safety, fairness, privacy protection, security, transparency,
accountability, education and literacy, fair competition, and innovation, restructured
from the 2019 Social Principles of Human-Centric AI. They are explicitly voluntary
("voluntarily take the necessary countermeasures"). The set adopts them as house
standards (policy clause 17) and draws the human-oversight, explanation, RAG and
generated-code points from them (clauses 10, 13.2, 14).

### 2.6 A binding amendment is decided but not enacted

The Personal Information Protection Commission's January 2026 triennial-review reform
policy states the amendments it will pursue and that "the amendment bill is expected to
be submitted to the Diet as early as spring 2026 and, if approved, would come into effect
approximately two years later." The AI-relevant items are: consent not required where
data is used solely to generate statistics, including AI development to the extent it is
statistics generation, for third-party provision and for acquiring published sensitive
information; rules for the personal data of persons under sixteen through their legal
representative with a best-interests provision; facial-feature-data duties, relaxed
cessation requirements and an opt-out-provision prohibition; relaxed data-subject
notification for low-risk breaches; a prohibition on inappropriate use and improper
acquisition of targeting information that is not personal information; and administrative
monetary penalties for serious, economically motivated violations. **The set relies on
none of it**; it takes the under-16 best-interests position and the facial-feature
caution as house standards because they are prudent, not because they bind. Policy
clauses 4.2, 7.4, 19.1; annex A3.

## 6. Points at which the set stops

- The exact leak-report timing, the opt-out filing particulars, the equivalent-measures
  standard and the disclosure procedure are in the Cabinet Order and Enforcement Rules;
  the set applies them by reference and does not restate figures that turn on the Rules.
- The pseudonymised- and anonymised-information regime (APPI Arts 41-46) is named and
  applied for the re-identification prohibition; its detailed processing standards are
  not restated.
- The 2026 amendment is described from the Commission's own reform policy; nothing in it
  is stated as a duty, and no enacted text is asserted.
- The set turns on the government's English translation; where a reading would depend on
  the precise Japanese wording, the set stays with the plain effect the translation
  gives and says the Japanese text governs.
