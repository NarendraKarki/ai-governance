# United Arab Emirates - AI Acceptable Use Policy set

**Status: PUBLISHED - verified within its stated scope: DIFC-registered
entities, with the ADGM and federal breach positions stated and nothing else
asserted about those regimes.** Every tier 1 citation was checked against the

**Educational, not legal advice** - review by qualified counsel before adoption.

## Purpose

An acceptable use policy for AI tools in a DIFC-registered entity, with an
enterprise governance annex, built on the one genuinely AI-specific instrument
in this series: the DIFC's accreditation and certification framework for
autonomous and semi-autonomous systems processing personal data. Obligations
are stated as rules a team can apply; provision identifiers live in the
research note, not the policy body.

Focus 5 of the series (implementing and operating).

## Why this set is different from every other in the series

Every other jurisdiction in this series reaches AI through general data
protection law. The DIFC addresses autonomous systems directly, and three
features follow:

1. **The UAE is three data protection jurisdictions, not one.** DIFC, ADGM
   and the federal regime have different regulators and three different
   answers to the same breach question - and the free zones are an exclusion
   from the federal law, not a variation on it. The policy routes each entity
   to its regime before anything else.
2. **The DIFC regime is a certification gate, not a documentation duty.** No
   person may make a System available for commercial use in High Risk
   Processing unless the Commissioner's audit and certification requirements
   are satisfied. The policy treats that as a gate to deployment.
3. **The boundary is autonomy, not automation.** Deterministic software is
   expressly out; a tool becomes a System the moment a vendor adds tool-use,
   retrieval, memory or planning. The policy turns that into a worked test,
   and the annex makes vendor release notes a governance input.

The regime also contemplates systems that define purposes for themselves -
requiring human-approved principles, stated limits, processing purpose logs
and algorithmic audits. Those requirements have no counterpart elsewhere in
this series and are carried in policy Section 11 and annex A6.

## Open gates (stated, not hidden)

1. **Regulation 10 itself and a current consolidation of the DIFC data
   protection law are not held.** Where the Framework quotes them, the set
   follows the quotation; where it only cites them, the set states the
   Framework's requirement and not the underlying provision.
2. **"High Risk Processing Activities" - the trigger for the whole regime -
   is not defined in the held Framework.** The policy routes the
   determination to the data protection lead in writing, with the cautious
   answer where uncertain.
3. **The DIFC consolidation previously verified is dated March 2022** and
   predates the 2023 amendments; the open-ended breach reading should be
   re-checked against a current consolidation.
4. **The federal Executive Regulations have not been issued** - no federal
   breach period exists, whatever commentary repeats to the contrary. The
   research note records the widely published 72-hour misattribution as a
   worked example of citation error.
5. **DFSA and FSRA rules are not held** - for a regulated firm the financial
   services rules may bite harder than data protection; no sector overlay
   exists.
