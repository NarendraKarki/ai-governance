Every document referred to, used, or deliberately not used in producing the UAE AI

## 0. Read this first

**The UAE is three data protection jurisdictions, not one.** DIFC, ADGM and the federal
regime have different regulators, different obligations and three different answers to
the same breach question. **The free zones are an exclusion from the federal regime, not
a variation on it.**

**This set is written for a DIFC-registered entity.** It states the ADGM and federal
breach positions because misapplying them is the most consequential error available in
this jurisdiction - and one that has been made in print. It states nothing else about
either regime.

**This set has two tiers of evidence and marks the seam between them.** Section 1 is held
and read in full. Section 2 was verified previously against official government sources
but is not present in this container. Everything downstream of that distinction is
labelled in the research note.

## 4. Two defects in the source, recorded

**4.1 The officer has two names.** The Framework calls the role the **"Automated Systems
Officer (ASO)"** where it defines it substantively (requirement 3.1) and the
**"Autonomous Systems Officer (ASO)"** in the audit criteria (4.3.5). This set uses the
first and records the variant, because an organisation searching its own documents for
one form will miss the other.

**4.2 A duplicated clause.** Audit criterion 4.3.5 ends with "...unless an exception to
this rule for extenuating circumstances is approved by the Commissioner" **twice**. The
requirement is unambiguous; the duplication is recorded so a reader comparing texts does
not infer two different exceptions. A similar artefact appears at 4.2 ("at least once in
during the three (3) year validity").

**Neither affects any obligation.** Both are recorded because a source defect that goes
unrecorded becomes a discrepancy someone later has to rediscover.

## 5. The issuer's own disclaimer

Every page of the Framework carries a notice stating that its content "is provided for
informational purposes only and **should not be considered complete, up to date or a
substitute for specific professional advice**".

**The issuing authority disclaims the completeness and currency of its own framework
document.** That is unusual enough to record here rather than only in the research note.
Annex A13.5 treats it as a standing reason to verify the current position before any
decision of consequence, and A4.5 requires the committee to record which version of the
Framework its position rests on.

## 10. The verifications that mattered most

### 10.1 Three regimes, three answers - and a well-documented error

The single most consequential thing to get right here is which regime governs, and the
breach clock is where getting it wrong does the most damage:

- **DIFC**: as soon as practicable, **no period at all**;
- **ADGM**: **72 hours** from awareness;
- **Federal**: **no period established**, because the Executive Regulations that would set
  it have not been issued.

A specific misattribution circulates widely and has appeared in professional commentary:
the phrase "without undue delay and, where feasible, not later than 72 hours after having
become aware of it", attributed to the **federal** law's breach article. That wording is
not in the federal decree-law. It is European provision wording, and the real 72-hour
duty sits in **ADGM**, whose regulations adopted similar language - which is why the
sentence matches something. **Commentary took a free-zone rule and attached a federal
article number to it.**

Related claims that fail against the official text: that the federal Executive
Regulations were issued in 2023 (they were not, and the claim never appears with a
Cabinet Decision number or on the official portal); that a specific maximum fine is set in
the decree-law (no amount is in it); and that a fixed subject-access response period
applies (none is specified). Several commonly cited article numbers for breach, impact
assessment and consent were also checked and found misattributed.

**This is worth recording as more than an anecdote.** It is a real, published instance of
a citation that verifies against a plausible secondary source and fails against the
primary text - the exact failure this programme's verification discipline exists to
catch, performed by humans, in print, before any AI tool was involved. Policy 18.5 exists
because of it, and annex A9.3 forbids using a 72-hour figure as the DIFC target by
default.

### 10.2 Autonomy, not automation, is the boundary

The Framework defines a **System** as a machine-based system operating in an **autonomous
or semi-autonomous** manner that processes personal data for human-defined purposes or
purposes it defines itself, and generates output on that basis - and expressly excludes
**"purely automated systems (i.e. systems which have no degree of autonomy in their
operation and whose operation is deterministically controlled by humans)"**, because the
underlying law already governs automated processing.

**This is not the boundary other frameworks draw**, and applying a familiar one would put
the wrong tools in scope and leave the right ones out. Policy Section 4 turns it into a
worked test, and 4.5 records the consequence an organisation is most likely to miss: **a
tool becomes a System without being replaced** the moment a vendor adds tool-use,
retrieval, memory or planning. Annex A2.6 makes vendor release notes a governance input.

### 10.3 The provision that has no counterpart anywhere else in this programme

Framework requirement 2.3: **"Any Self-defined purposes generated by the System, i.e.
automated decision-making, shall conform to a predefined set of human approved
principles."**

The evidence it asks for is more specific than the requirement: **code review records
addressing "the absence of self-defined purposes"**; **processing purpose logs** tracking
what the System actually processed for; and **algorithmic audits** assessing the
algorithms against the human-defined purposes with corrective actions taken. The notice
particulars go further still, requiring disclosure of "**all human-defined limits within
which, the System is capable of itself defining further purposes for Processing of
Personal Data**".

Three consequences the set draws, none of which would appear in a policy written from a
general data protection law:

- **Processing purpose logging is a design requirement**, and a System that cannot produce
  such a log is not approvable for High Risk Processing (policy 11.5, annex A6.3);
- **a code review that confirms the intended purposes without addressing whether others
  can be generated is incomplete** (annex A6.5);
- **processing for a purpose outside the approved set is an incident, not a finding**
  (annex A6.6, A9.6).

### 10.4 The constraint most likely to be breached by accident

Audit criterion 4.3.5: **no more than a one-month gap** before the operation of a System
for High Risk Processing, or **between an outgoing ASO and a replacement**, absent an
exception approved by the Commissioner.

An ordinary resignation breaches this. Policy 12.4 and annex A5.2-A5.4 therefore require a
named deputy at all times, a succession process **triggered on the day notice is given
rather than the day of departure**, and a metric counting days of vacancy - with an
overrun reported to the accountable executive as a compliance failure, not a recruitment
delay.

### 10.5 Evidence that cannot be produced retrospectively

Framework requirement 1.2's acceptable evidence includes an impact assessment that
"includes management actions plans to address how controls have been integrated into
development process **(and evidence that controls have been integrated)**".

**That cannot be met by a document written after deployment.** Combined with the
Commissioner's discretion over the implementation timeline, it produces the position at
policy 21.2 and annex A4.4: readiness is not deferred until enforcement begins, because
the evidence the scheme requires takes longer to build than the certification takes to
apply for, and it has to be built during development or not at all.
