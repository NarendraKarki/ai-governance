# Singapore - AI Acceptable Use Policy set

**Status: PUBLISHED - verified against the enacted Act; gates stated below.**
Every citation resolves to the Personal Data Protection Act 2012 as amended,
read directly; where a figure or threshold lives in subordinate legislation
that is not held, the set names the gap and routes the judgement instead of
filling it from any other source.
**Educational, not legal advice** - review by qualified counsel before adoption.

## Purpose

An acceptable use policy for AI tools in an organisation under Singapore
personal data protection law, with an enterprise governance annex.
Obligations are stated as rules a team can apply; section numbers live in the
research note, not the policy body.

Focus 5 of the series (implementing and operating).

## What shapes the Singapore edition

1. **There is no AI statute, and the set says so.** The Act is
   technology-neutral - a full-text search returns no occurrence of
   "artificial intelligence", "automated decision", "profiling" or
   "algorithm". Every AI obligation reaches AI as a personal data
   obligation, and every clause that goes further is labelled a house
   standard in the policy text itself.
2. **An individual can be prosecuted personally.** Unauthorised disclosure
   or use of personal data, and unauthorised re-identification, are offences
   carrying imprisonment - and the offences turn on what the *organisation*
   authorised. That converts this acceptable use policy from an internal
   control document into the instrument that sets the boundary of an
   individual's criminal exposure. Policy Section 11 is built on it, and no
   exception may blur the boundary.
3. **AI-inferred data about a person is accessible but not correctable** -
   the statutory correction right excepts derived personal data while the
   access right does not. The set records the asymmetry and then declines to
   rely on the exception, as a stated house standard.
4. **The breach clock runs from the assessment, not from discovery** - three
   calendar days from the notifiability assessment, with a separate duty to
   assess expeditiously. The policy states both halves, because either one
   alone misleads.

## Open gates (stated, not hidden)

1. **The Personal Data Protection Regulations 2021 are not held - the
   largest gap.** They carry the breach-notifiability thresholds, the
   prescribed transfer requirements, the access-rejection time, the
   preservation period, and the means of derivation prescribed out of
   "derived personal data". The set names each obligation and routes the
   figures to the data protection lead rather than stating them.
2. **The PDPC's AI advisory guidelines and Model AI Governance Framework are
   not held.** The Commission has stated how it interprets the Act for AI
   systems; nothing here asserts that content. Obtaining the guidelines is
   the highest-value next verification for this set.
3. **Part 6B and the Twelfth Schedule are enforced but absent** - the Act
   cross-references a Part its enacted text does not carry. The likely
   explanation (the data portability regime, enacted but not commenced) is
   marked as an unverified inference, because verifying it needs the
   Amendment Act and commencement notifications, which are not held.
4. **No sector regulator instrument is held** - no technology-risk or
   outsourcing overlay exists.
