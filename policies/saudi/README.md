# Saudi Arabia - AI Acceptable Use Policy set

**Status: WORKING DRAFT - government data only.** Verified against the three NDMO
instruments held. **The Kingdom's Personal Data Protection Law and its implementing
regulations are not held.** This set cannot serve a private organisation processing
its own customers' or employees' personal data, and it does not pretend to. Read
section 1.1 of the policy before anything else.

## Purpose

An acceptable use policy for AI tools in a public entity or business partner handling
Saudi government data, with an enterprise governance annex, built from the national
data management, classification and sharing standards. Obligations are stated as rules
a team can apply; control and specification identifiers live in the research note, not
the policy body.

Focus 5 of the series (implementing and operating).

## Scope - read this first

This set applies to **public entities** and to **business partners handling government
data**. It does not apply to a private organisation processing only its own
commercial data. The three instruments above say so expressly and consistently; the
policy repeats it at section 1.1, the annex at A13, and the source register at
section 0.

## Three routes the instruments carry AI

The NDMO instruments contain no AI provision. Every AI obligation in the policy was
traced to an existing one. Three carry real weight:

1. **Classification.** Where a dataset combines levels, the highest level governs
   the whole. A corpus, a fine-tuning set, a multi-document prompt, or an index is
   a combined dataset; the most restricted element sets the classification.
2. **The yearly personal data risk assessment.** It reaches information systems
   "whether automated or manual", which carries AI systems in without AI-specific
   language.
3. **Data sharing.** A commercial AI service receiving government data is a
   non-government data requester. The agreement contents prescribed by the Data
   Sharing Policy map almost exactly onto what an AI vendor contract must settle:
   purpose limitation, territorial limits, third-party restrictions, and a
   destruction mechanism.

Everything else in the policy that goes beyond these three is a house standard,
labelled as such where it appears.

## Open gates (stated, not hidden)

1. **The Personal Data Protection Law and its implementing regulations are not held.**
   The set cannot state the lawful bases for processing personal data, the conditions
   for transferring personal data outside the Kingdom, or any penalty. Those
   instruments close all of it.
2. **The NDMO Personal Data Protection Regulations are not held.** Nine of the ten
   PDP specifications direct the reader to them. The set states what the standards
   themselves require and stops where they point elsewhere.
3. **NCA cybersecurity specifications are not held.** The Data Security domain is
   carved out of the standards entirely and deferred to the National Cybersecurity
   Authority. Policy section 13 states house standards and directs the reader to NCA
   requirements without asserting them.
4. **The NDMO Organizational Manual is not held.** Role responsibilities at annex A1
   must be aligned to it; where it differs, it governs.
5. **No sector regulator instrument** is held; no sector overlay exists.
