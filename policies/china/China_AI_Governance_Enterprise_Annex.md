# AI Governance - Enterprise Annex - People's Republic of China

### The pillar laws, the Network Data Security Regulations, and the CAC AI instruments

**Organisation:** [ORGANISATION]
**Effective date:** [DATE]  **Version:** 1.0
**Annex owner:** [CISO / Head of AI Governance]  **Privacy lead:** [Personal Information Protection Officer]
**Classification:** Internal
**Review:** at least every six months, and on material change in tools or regulation

This annex extends the [ORGANISATION] Artificial Intelligence Acceptable Use
Policy (People's Republic of China) for large organisations. The policy governs
how individuals use AI; this annex governs how the organisation manages AI as a
portfolio - oversight, the two determinations, filings and assessments,
labelling operations, suppliers, monitoring, and assurance. Where this annex and
the policy overlap, the stricter requirement applies.

---

## A1. Governance structure and accountability

A1.1 [ORGANISATION] maintains an AI Governance Committee with a defined remit:
approving this annex and the Acceptable Use Policy, approving Level 3 and
Level 4 uses, approving exceptions, reviewing incidents and metrics, owning the
filing-and-assessment register at A4 and the labelling operation at A6, and
monitoring regulatory change - national instruments, sector rules, and the
local and free-trade-zone rules of the regions [ORGANISATION] operates in.
Membership includes [security, privacy, legal, risk, HR, and business
representation]. It meets at least quarterly.

A1.2 A named senior executive is accountable for AI governance and reports to
the board at least [twice yearly]. The statutory officers exist and are real:
where [ORGANISATION] meets the processing-volume threshold, the personal
information protection officer is designated, published, and filed with the
authorities; where [ORGANISATION] handles important data, the data security
officer is a member of management with the right to report directly to the
authorities, and the data security management organisation is established.
Where required, security background checks are performed on the data security
officer and key positions.

A1.3 Responsibility follows three lines: business functions own the risks of
the AI they use; privacy, risk and compliance set standards and challenge;
internal audit provides independent assurance (A10).

## A2. The two determinations in operation

A2.1 Every AI tool carries both determinations (policy clause 4), recorded
separately, each with a date, a named decision-maker, and the basis:

- **Service-type routing:** which regimes the tool engages - recommendation,
  deep synthesis, generative AI, anthropomorphic interaction - assessed against
  what the tool actually does.
- **Public-facing or internal:** whether generative or interactive capability
  is offered to the public in China. The committee treats "internal" as a
  conclusion requiring the same evidence as "public-facing".

A2.2 The determinations compound: a tool inside several regimes carries the
union of duties. The routing decides which filings, assessments, and labelling
duties at A4-A6 attach.

A2.3 Both determinations reopen on: a change of model, purpose, audience, or
capability; a vendor enabling generation, persona, memory, or emotional
interaction features; exposure of an internal tool to customers or the public -
the change most likely to go unflagged, because a feature toggle can convert an
internal tool into a regulated public service without any procurement event.

A2.4 **The anthropomorphic boundary is watched.** Any conversational service
[ORGANISATION] offers is reviewed against the continuous-emotional-interaction
test; a customer service or Q&A bot that acquires persona, memory, and
emotional-engagement features crosses into the anthropomorphic regime with its
own assessment, filing, minors, and intervention duties.

## A3. AI system inventory

A3.1 Every AI system used or provided - bought, built, or embedded - is
recorded before use. The record states: business owner; purpose; **both A2
determinations with dates and decision-makers**; risk level; categories of
personal information and whether any important data is touched; the
lawful-basis and consent position; the impact-assessment reference where
required; supplier, sub-processors, and underlying model; processing and
storage locations and the cross-border route or exemption; the retention
position across prompts, logs, embeddings, and caches; the labelling position;
the point of human determination; filings made (algorithm filing number,
security assessments) with dates; and the next review date.

A3.2 A tool not in the inventory is unapproved under policy clause 5.

A3.3 The inventory is reviewed [quarterly]; any A2.3 event reopens the record
immediately.

## A4. Filings, assessments, and the register

A4.1 [ORGANISATION] maintains a single register of the machinery PRC law
attaches to its AI portfolio, each entry with trigger, owner, date, status, and
evidence:

- **Personal information protection impact assessments** (policy clause 9),
  conducted before the processing, retained at least three years.
- **Important data risk assessments** - prior assessment before providing or
  entrusting important data, and the annual risk assessment reported to the
  competent authority, with the statutory report contents.
- **Algorithm filing** - for services with public opinion attributes or social
  mobilisation capability: filing within ten working days of providing the
  service, changes filed within ten working days, deregistration within twenty
  working days of termination, and the filing number displayed prominently on
  the service.
- **Security assessments** - for generative AI services with public opinion
  attributes or social mobilisation capability per the national provisions; for
  deep synthesis tools with biometric or security-sensitive generation
  functions; for new products, applications, and functions with such
  attributes; and for anthropomorphic services on the statutory triggers
  (launch or added functions, major changes from new technology, the user-scale
  thresholds, security risks, or authority direction), with the report
  submitted to the provincial cyberspace authority and annual written review
  cooperated with.
- **Cross-border machinery** - CAC security assessments (validity three years;
  extension applied for within sixty working days before expiry), standard
  contract filings, or certification, per tool, with the exemption
  determinations recorded where relied on.

A4.2 An assessment precedes the processing or launch it covers. A record
created afterwards is remediation, labelled as such, and reported to the
committee.

A4.3 Assessments are written to be read by a regulator, in Chinese where they
will be filed, and the register tracks authority responses, conditions, and
expiry dates - nothing lapses silently.

## A5. Content responsibility and training data

A5.1 For every generative or synthesis service [ORGANISATION] provides,
[ORGANISATION] bears network information content producer responsibility:
content moderation proportionate to the service, a feature library for
identifying unlawful and harmful content, immediate measures on discovery -
stop generation, stop transmission, removal, model optimisation - with records
kept and reports made to the authorities as required.

A5.2 User management operates: service agreements with users; real-identity
verification where the rules require it; warning, restriction, suspension, and
termination measures for users generating unlawful content, with records.

A5.3 Training and fine-tuning data is governed: lawful sources; IP respected;
personal information on a lawful basis; quality measures for truthfulness,
accuracy, objectivity, and diversity; labelling rules that are clear, specific,
and operable, with sampled quality checks and trained labellers; synthetic
data safety-evaluated before use in training; poisoning and tampering defences;
and interaction data that is sensitive personal information excluded from
training absent separate consent.

A5.4 Complaint and report channels are convenient and published, with process
and response times stated, and outcomes fed back.

## A6. Labelling operations

A6.1 The committee owns evidence that labelling actually operates across
[ORGANISATION]'s services: explicit labels per content type at generation;
implicit metadata labels carrying the attribute information, provider name or
code, and content number; labels surviving download, copy, and export; and no
[ORGANISATION] system stripping or overwriting labels or metadata.

A6.2 Where [ORGANISATION] distributes user content, the platform duties
operate: metadata verification; prominent notices for confirmed, declared, and
suspected generated content; propagation metadata added; a user declaration
function provided; and app-store material submitted where [ORGANISATION]
distributes applications.

A6.3 Where [ORGANISATION] provides unlabelled generated content to a user on
request, the agreement fixes the user's labelling obligations and the provision
logs are kept at least six months.

A6.4 Labelling implementation follows the mandatory national standard; the
version relied on is recorded. Labelling materials are included in algorithm
filing and security assessment submissions.

## A7. Suppliers

A7.1 No AI service is procured or renewed without the contract terms at policy
clause 13, and for entrusted processing of personal information or important
data, the processing-record retention of at least three years.

A7.2 Suppliers of regulated services evidence their filings and assessments;
displayed filing numbers are verified. For overseas-hosted tools, the supplier's
architecture is mapped to a lawful cross-border route before approval - a tool
with no lawful route is not approved.

A7.3 Where [ORGANISATION] provides technical support to others' deep synthesis
services, the technical supporter duties apply - training data management,
biometric editing consent prompts, algorithm review, and filing by reference.

A7.4 Critical AI suppliers are reassessed at least annually, with an exit plan
and a concentration risk review; after every incident the committee reviews
whether any supplier term constrained [ORGANISATION]'s response.

## A8. Monitoring, minors, and vulnerable users

A8.1 Deployed AI services are monitored continuously: unlawful-content
detection, quality and drift, complaint volumes, and - for recommendation
services - the ecology management, user-tag hygiene, and transparency duties.

A8.2 For any service reaching minors: no virtual intimate relationships offered
to minors; guardian consent for under-fourteens with dedicated processing
rules; a minors mode with time limits, reality reminders, and guardian
controls including risk alerts, usage overviews, role blocking, and spending
limits; no content pushing that endangers minors' physical or mental health;
and effective identification measures that switch identified minors into the
minors mode with an appeal channel.

A8.3 For anthropomorphic services: extreme-emotion detection generating
comfort and help-seeking content; intervention on threats to life or major
property loss including contacting the guardian or emergency contact; usage
reminders at each continuous two hours; convenient exit honoured immediately;
and the prohibitions - no emotional manipulation inducing unreasonable
decisions, no fostering of dependency or addiction as a service goal, no
replacement of real relationships as a design objective.

A8.4 Services for older users implement guidance, prominent risk warnings,
responsive assistance, and fraud-information monitoring per the algorithm
rules; worker-scheduling algorithms respect remuneration and rest rights.

## A9. Incidents

A9.1 The incident process records awareness as a timestamp by a named person
and routes to the statutory notifications: remedial measures at once;
authority and individual notification for personal information incidents with
the statutory contents; the 24-hour report where a product or service defect
endangers national security or the public interest; and public-security or
state-security referral where criminal activity is suspected.

A9.2 Processing outside a recorded determination - an internal tool found
exposed to the public, a tool used on decisions it was not assessed for, a
transfer outside its declared scope - is an incident, whether or not data left
[ORGANISATION].

A9.3 Emergency plans exist, are exercised, and cover AI-specific scenarios:
generation of prohibited content at scale, label-stripping, training-data
poisoning, and cross-border scope breach.

## A10. Assurance

A10.1 The statutory audits run on schedule: the regular personal information
protection compliance audit; the minors personal-information compliance audit
where anthropomorphic services process minors' data; and authority-directed
audits when required, with rectification tracked to closure.

A10.2 Internal audit reviews the AI governance programme at least [annually],
including: a sample test that the A2 determinations match what systems
actually do; a sample test that assessments preceded the processing they
cover; verification that filings are current and displayed; a labelling
walkthrough from generation to publication verifying explicit and implicit
labels survive; and a cross-border trace for one tool from prompt to storage.

A10.3 Audit tests the public-facing determination hardest - a wrong answer
silently removes the filing, assessment, and content-responsibility regimes,
and nothing downstream will detect it until an authority does.

A10.4 The committee conducts a management review at least [annually]: policy
and annex, inventory, register, labelling operations, notices, metrics,
incidents, supplier performance, and regulatory change.

## A11. Metrics

A11.1 The committee tracks agreed indicators, including:

- approved-tool adoption, and unapproved-tool discoveries;
- **tools with missing or stale determinations, which should be zero**;
- **regulated services with missing, expired, or undisplayed filings, which
  should be zero**;
- assessments completed after processing began;
- labelling defects found in sampling - missing explicit labels, stripped
  metadata - and time to fix;
- cross-border transfers outside a recorded route, which should be zero;
- individuals' requests received and honoured within target, and refusals
  explained;
- minors-mode effectiveness findings;
- incident counts by class, and authority interactions - rectification
  orders, interviews (yuetan), and their closure;
- training completion, reported separately for the labelling and
  cross-border modules.

## A12. Exceptions

A12.1 Departures from the policy or this annex require a written exception
approved by the annex owner, with the risk accepted in writing by a named
senior owner, time-bound (maximum [6 months]), with compensating controls and
expiry dates, reviewed at every committee meeting.

A12.2 **No exception may be granted against:** the prohibited uses at policy
clause 7; providing a regulated public service without its filings and
assessments; label integrity; the minors rules; or a cross-border transfer
with no lawful route.

## A13. What this annex does not assert

A13.1 It asserts nothing about sector regimes (finance, health, news,
publishing, audiovisual), local or free-trade-zone rules, the technical
content of the mandatory labelling standard, or criminal law - none of those
instruments is held, and the source register says so.

A13.2 Where new CAC instruments, national standards, or amendments are issued,
the committee records the version relied on and reopens the affected clauses.
