# Research note - People's Republic of China AI policy set

Basis for the [ORGANISATION] AI Acceptable Use Policy (China) v1.0 and the
Enterprise Annex v1.0. Provision identifiers live here, not in the policy body.
Verification was performed against the Chinese texts in the source register -
PRC law is enacted in Chinese, and no official English translation exists for
any instrument in this set. State as at 3 September 2026.

---

## 1. The instruments this set rests on

Three pillar laws of the National People's Congress Standing Committee:

- **Cybersecurity Law** (网络安全法), adopted 7 November 2016, in force 1 June
  2017, **as amended by the NPCSC decision of 28 October 2025** - the held text
  is the amended consolidation, 81 articles.
- **Data Security Law** (数据安全法), adopted 10 June 2021, in force
  1 September 2021, 55 articles.
- **Personal Information Protection Law** (个人信息保护法, "PIPL"), adopted
  20 August 2021, in force 1 November 2021, 74 articles.

One State Council administrative regulation:

- **Network Data Security Management Regulations** (网络数据安全管理条例),
  dated 24 September 2024, in force 1 January 2025, 64 articles - the
  operational layer across all three pillar laws.

Six departmental rules and normative documents of the Cyberspace
Administration of China (CAC) and co-issuing ministries:

- **Algorithm Recommendation Provisions** (互联网信息服务算法推荐管理规定),
  CAC/MIIT/MPS/SAMR Order No. 9, in force 1 March 2022, 35 articles.
- **Deep Synthesis Provisions** (互联网信息服务深度合成管理规定),
  CAC/MIIT/MPS Order No. 12, in force 10 January 2023, 25 articles.
- **Generative AI Interim Measures** (生成式人工智能服务管理暂行办法),
  seven-department Order No. 15, in force 15 August 2023, 24 articles.
- **Cross-border Data Flow Provisions** (促进和规范数据跨境流动规定),
  CAC Order No. 16, in force on publication 22 March 2024, 14 articles.
- **AI-Generated Content Labelling Measures** (人工智能生成合成内容标识办法),
  issued by notice Guo Xin Ban Tong Zi [2025] No. 2 of 7 March 2025, in force
  1 September 2025, 14 articles.
- **Anthropomorphic Interaction Interim Measures**
  (人工智能拟人化互动服务管理暂行办法), five-department Order No. 21, adopted
  2 February 2026, published 10 April 2026, **in force 15 July 2026**,
  32 articles - the newest AI instrument in this series.

## 2. The structural finding - a layered regime routed by service type

China regulates AI by regulating service types, in the order the technology
arrived: recommendation algorithms (2022), deep synthesis (2023), generative
AI (2023), labelling (2025), anthropomorphic interaction (2026) - all resting
on the pillar laws and the 2025 data regulations. The instruments
cross-reference each other deliberately: the generative AI measures route
labelling through the deep synthesis provisions (GenAI Art 12) and filing
through the algorithm provisions (GenAI Art 17); the labelling measures
implement deep synthesis Arts 16 and 17 (Label Arts 4-5); the anthropomorphic
measures route filing the same way (Anthro Art 26). No single instrument
states a complete duty - the policy therefore routes every tool through
service-type determinations rather than through any one instrument.

## 4. The findings worth recording

### 4.1 The amendment that moved the articles

The 2025 amendment to the Cybersecurity Law did not only raise penalties -
it **renumbered the law**. Two articles were inserted (a leadership clause as
Art 3 and a new AI clause as Art 20), taking the law from 79 to 81 articles
and shifting most citations by one or two. The multi-level protection scheme
duties universally cited for eight years as "CSL Art 21" now sit at
**Art 23**; the breach-response duty cited as Art 25 now sits at Art 27; the
penalty ceilings in the liability chapter rose sharply (general operator
violations now reach RMB 2,000,000, and especially severe consequences
RMB 10,000,000 at Art 61 para 3). Any compliance document citing
pre-amendment article numbers now points at the wrong provisions. This set
cites the amended consolidation only. This is the citation-trap variant of
the commencement trap recorded for Colorado, India, and the EU. One register
caveat: the amending decision is held only as consolidated into the text; the
decision's own commencement date is not asserted from the held corpus, and
nothing in this set depends on it.

### 4.2 The Cybersecurity Law now contains an AI article

New Art 20, inserted by the 2025 amendment: the state supports AI basic
research and key technology development, advances training-data and
computing-power infrastructure, **improves AI ethical norms, and strengthens
risk monitoring, assessment, and safety regulation** to promote AI
application and healthy development. It is a state-policy provision, not an
operator duty - but it puts AI governance into the text of a pillar law for
the first time, and it is the statutory peg for the ethics-review and
risk-assessment language that runs through the CAC instruments.

### 4.3 The public-facing line is the whole game for enterprises

GenAI Art 2 para 3 excludes from the generative AI measures organisations
that develop and apply the technology **without providing services to the
domestic public**. An enterprise using AI internally is governed by the
pillar laws and the data regulations - demanding but familiar territory. The
moment generative capability is exposed to the Chinese public, the provider
regime engages: content producer responsibility (Art 9), security assessment
and algorithm filing (Art 17), labelling (Art 12), minors protection
(Art 10). The policy makes this a recorded determination per tool
(Determination B), and the annex treats an internal tool exposed to the
public without its filings as an incident. This is the single scoping rule
most often missed in secondary coverage of the Chinese regime.

### 4.4 Labelling is one duty spread across four instruments

No single instrument states the complete labelling duty. Deep synthesis
Arts 16-17 create implicit and explicit labelling; the Labelling Measures
Arts 4-5 specify both by content type and fix the metadata elements; AlgoRec
Art 9 makes platforms re-label unlabelled synthetic content before further
transmission; GenAI Art 12 routes generative providers into the deep
synthesis labelling duty; and Label Art 11 pulls in a mandatory national
standard (named in the source register as not held). Citing any one
instrument alone yields an incomplete obligation - the policy states the
merged duty and this note holds the map.

### 4.5 The anthropomorphic measures are new law with hard edges

In force 15 July 2026, the measures contain the sharpest bright-line rules
in the set: **no virtual relatives or virtual partners for minors at all**
(Art 14 para 1 - not an age-gate, a prohibition); guardian consent for
under-fourteens for any anthropomorphic service; a usage reminder at every
continuous two hours (Art 18); mandatory intervention, including contacting a
guardian or emergency contact, where a user faces major property loss or
expresses intent to self-harm (Art 13); a bar on using sensitive-PI
interaction data for model training without separate consent (Art 16); and
security-assessment triggers at 1,000,000 registered or 100,000 monthly
active users (Art 22). The service-agreement requirement extends to
collecting age, guardian, or emergency contact information (Art 12). Most
enterprises will sit outside this regime - the exclusions for customer
service, Q&A, and work assistants are explicit - but the boundary is
functional, not nominal, and the annex watches it (A2.4).

### 4.6 The cross-border thresholds count from 1 January

The 2024 Cross-border Provisions replaced the earlier threshold architecture:
exemption under 100,000 individuals' non-sensitive personal information,
standard contract or certification from 100,000 to 1,000,000 (or under
10,000 sensitive), security assessment at 1,000,000+ (or 10,000+ sensitive) -
all counted **cumulatively from 1 January of the current year** (Arts 5, 7,
8), with contract-necessity, HR, and emergency exemptions and free trade zone
negative lists. Art 13 expressly prevails over the 2022 assessment measures
and 2023 standard contract measures where they conflict. The relief in Art 2
matters operationally: data not notified or published as important data need
not be declared for assessment as important data - an enterprise is not
required to guess.

### 4.7 The foreign-authority block reaches AI vendors

PIPL Art 41 and DSL Art 36 prohibit providing data stored in China to
foreign judicial or law enforcement authorities without PRC approval - with
dedicated penalties (DSL Art 48 para 2). An overseas AI vendor's standard
legal-process response is exactly this scenario. The policy therefore makes
supplier legal-process terms a contracting point (Section 13) and prohibits
such provision without approval (7.8). This is the same exposure the Saudi
set recorded from Implementing Regulation Art 17(1)(e), arrived at from the
opposite direction - there a contracting duty, here a statutory prohibition.

### 4.8 The PIPIA trigger includes automated decision-making

PIPL Art 55 requires a prior personal information protection impact
assessment for automated decision-making as such - alongside sensitive
personal information, provision and publication, and cross-border transfers -
with reports and records kept three years (Art 56). Practically every
consequential enterprise AI use in China therefore carries a prior,
documented assessment duty. The parallel with Bahrain (where the DPIA
trigger was found in the Arabic of Order 43) is recorded in the series
comparison work.

## 5. Not held

Stated here so nothing is silently assumed:

- **The mandatory national labelling standard** (named by Label Art 11) -
  its technical content is not asserted; the annex requires recording the
  version relied on.
- **The 2025 CSL amending decision as a standalone text** - held only as
  consolidated; its commencement date is not asserted.
- **The 2022 Data Export Security Assessment Measures and 2023 Standard
  Contract Measures** - modified by the held 2024 Provisions (Art 13); the
  assessment and contract procedures' detail is not asserted.
- **Sector rules** - finance, health, news services, publishing,
  audiovisual; engaged by GenAI Art 2 para 2, DeepSynth Art 24, Anthro
  Art 31 and named as applying in addition.
- **Local and free-trade-zone rules**, including FTZ negative lists
  (Xborder Art 6).
- **The Science and Technology Progress Law** (named in GenAI Art 1),
  the Minors Network Protection Regulations (named in Anthro Art 1), the
  Civil Code, and the criminal law dimension.
- **CII designation rules** - whether an organisation is a critical
  information infrastructure operator is determined under the CII
  Regulations, not held; the set states the consequences, not the
  designation.
- **Enforcement decisions and case law** - none relied on anywhere.

## 6. What this set therefore does not say

No breach deadline beyond those read in the held texts (the 24-hour
defect-report rule and the statutory notice contents) is stated. No sector
obligation is stated. No FTZ negative-list position is stated. Nothing is
asserted about the labelling standard's technical requirements, about
assessment procedure detail under the 2022-2023 measures, or about any
instrument in Chinese legal history not listed in the source register.

## 7. Method

Every provision cited resolves to text in a held Chinese file. The
verification reads were performed on the Chinese texts directly - there is
no translation tier in this jurisdiction because no official translation
exists; unofficial translations were not used at all. One extraction note:
the CAC print-to-PDF fonts encode certain characters as Unicode Kangxi
radical variants; verification applied NFKC normalisation before article
mapping, and article-by-article continuity was verified for all ten
instruments (74, 81, 55, 64, 24, 25, 35, 14, 14, and 32 articles
respectively, no gaps). Hashes were taken of the files exactly as supplied.
Web search was used to locate official download pages - existence and
location only, never authority.
