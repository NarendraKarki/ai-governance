# Artificial Intelligence Acceptable Use Policy - People's Republic of China

### The three pillar laws, the Network Data Security Regulations, and the six CAC AI instruments

**Organisation:** [ORGANISATION]
**Effective date:** [DATE]  **Version:** 1.0
**Policy owner:** [CISO / Head of AI Governance]  **Privacy lead:** [Personal Information Protection Officer]
**Classification:** Internal
**Review:** at least annually, and on material change in tools or regulation

*This document is an educational policy template and does not constitute legal
advice. It reflects obligations under the PRC instruments set out in the

adoption. PRC law is enacted in Chinese; this policy was verified against the

## 1. Purpose

This policy governs how personnel of [ORGANISATION] use artificial intelligence
tools - generative AI assistants, coding assistants, embedded AI features,
recommendation and decision-support systems, and AI services offered to
[ORGANISATION]'s own users - in a manner consistent with the law of the People's
Republic of China.

Three features of the Chinese regime shape everything in this policy.

### 1.1 The regime is layered, and the layers interlock

China has no single AI act. It has three pillar laws - the Cybersecurity Law as
amended in 2025, the Data Security Law, and the Personal Information Protection
Law - a State Council regulation that operationalises all three (the Network Data
Security Management Regulations), and a stack of technology-specific rules from
the Cyberspace Administration of China and co-issuing ministries: algorithm
recommendation, deep synthesis, generative AI, AI content labelling, cross-border
data flows, and - newest of all, in force since July 2026 - anthropomorphic
interaction services. A single AI tool can sit inside several of these regimes at
once, and the obligations compound. This policy routes every tool through the
question: **which service types does it provide?**

### 1.2 The most important scoping line is public-facing or not

The generative AI rules attach to services **provided to the public inside
China**. Research, development, and internal enterprise use that does not offer
generative capability to the domestic public sit outside the generative AI
measures - but remain fully inside the pillar laws and the data regulations. The
moment [ORGANISATION] exposes a generative feature to the Chinese public, an
entirely different tier of duties engages: content responsibility, security
assessment, algorithm filing, labelling. Clause 4 makes this determination
mandatory and recorded for every tool.

### 1.3 Labelling is law, not etiquette

Since September 2025, AI-generated and synthesised content must carry explicit
labels (visible to people) and implicit labels (embedded in file metadata), under
a dedicated labelling regime backed by a mandatory national standard. Removing,
altering, or forging labels is prohibited - for everyone, including users of AI
tools. This policy treats label integrity as a personal obligation of every
member of staff.

## 2. Scope

2.1 This policy applies to all personnel - employees, contractors, interns, and
temporary staff - and to all use of AI tools in connection with [ORGANISATION]'s
business where PRC law applies: activities inside mainland China, the processing
of personal information of natural persons located in China for the purposes the
law names, and services offered to the public in China.

2.2 It covers AI tools in every form: public chatbots and assistants, enterprise
AI subscriptions, AI features embedded in approved software, coding assistants,
recommendation systems, decision-support and screening systems, and AI services
[ORGANISATION] itself provides to users.

2.3 This policy is the general baseline. The Enterprise Annex governs
organisation-level duties - filings, assessments, labelling operations,
suppliers, monitoring. Where sector rules apply to [ORGANISATION] (finance,
health, news, publishing, audiovisual services), the sector regulator's
requirements apply in addition.

## 3. Which law reaches which use

3.1 **All use of AI tools** sits under the pillar laws: network and data security
duties, the prohibition on unlawful content, and - wherever personal information
is involved - the Personal Information Protection Law in full.

3.2 **Recommendation and ranking.** A tool that pushes, ranks, filters, or
schedules information to users through algorithms engages the algorithm
recommendation rules: transparency to users, an option to switch personalised
recommendation off, user tag deletion, and protections for minors, older people,
workers under algorithmic scheduling, and consumers against differential pricing.

3.3 **Deep synthesis.** A tool that generates or materially edits text, voice,
faces, images, video, or immersive scenes engages the deep synthesis rules:
content review, real-identity verification of users, labelling, and - for
biometric editing functions - the informed separate consent of the person whose
face or voice is edited.

3.4 **Generative AI provided to the public.** Offering generative capability to
the public in China engages the generative AI measures: lawful training data,
content responsibility for what the service generates, minors protections,
complaint handling, and the security-assessment and filing duties for services
with public opinion attributes or social mobilisation capability.

3.5 **Anthropomorphic interaction.** A service that simulates a natural person's
personality, thinking, and communication style for continuous emotional
interaction - companionship, emotional support, persona chat - engages the 2026
anthropomorphic interaction measures: emotional-safety duties, intervention in
extreme situations, strict minors rules, usage-time reminders, exit rights, and
security assessment. Customer service bots, knowledge Q&A, work assistants, and
educational tools without continuous emotional interaction are outside this
regime; clause 4 records the determination either way.

3.6 **Cross-border.** Any AI use that moves personal information or important
data out of mainland China - including prompts sent to an overseas-hosted model -
engages the cross-border regime in clause 10.

## 4. The two determinations

4.1 Every AI tool is assessed and recorded, before approval and at every reopen
event, on two determinations:

**Determination A - service-type routing.** Which of the regimes in clause 3
the tool engages: recommendation, deep synthesis, generative AI, anthropomorphic
interaction - or none of them beyond the baseline. The determination is made
against what the tool actually does, not what it is called.

**Determination B - public-facing or internal.** Whether the capability is
offered to the public in China, or confined to internal use. The public-facing
answer brings the generative AI measures, the filing and security-assessment
machinery, and content responsibility for output. "Internal" is a conclusion
requiring the same evidence as "public-facing": a feature exposed in a
customer-facing app, a public demo, or an API resold onward is public-facing.

4.2 Both determinations are recorded with a date, a named decision-maker, and the
basis, and are reopened on any change of model, purpose, audience, or capability
- in particular when an internal tool is exposed to customers, which changes
almost every obligation that applies to it.

## 5. Approved tools

5.1 Personnel may use only AI tools approved by [ORGANISATION] and listed in the
AI inventory. Using an unapproved tool for [ORGANISATION] business - including
free public chatbots on personal accounts - is a policy violation.

5.2 Approval requires: the two determinations at clause 4; supplier checks at
clause 13; where the tool is supplied from outside mainland China, the
cross-border analysis at clause 10; and, for tools [ORGANISATION] provides to
the public, the filings and assessments the Enterprise Annex governs.

5.3 Personnel must use approved tools through [ORGANISATION] accounts, not
personal accounts, so that contractual protections, retention settings, and
audit apply.

## 6. Levels of use

6.1 [ORGANISATION] classifies each use of AI at one of four levels. The levels
are [ORGANISATION]'s own classification - they are anchored to the statutory
triggers of PRC law, so that moving up a level engages the controls the law
itself requires.

**Level 1 - General assistance.** No personal information, no confidential
data, no output published or sent outside [ORGANISATION] without review.
Drafting, summarising public material, general research, code assistance on
non-sensitive code.

**Level 2 - Personal information involved.** Any prompt, upload, or output that
contains personal information engages the Personal Information Protection Law:
lawful basis, minimum necessity, notice, and the impact-assessment triggers at
clause 9. Requires an approved tool with a data processing agreement.

**Level 3 - Sensitive personal information, important data, or cross-border.**
Sensitive personal information (biometrics, religious belief, specific identity,
medical health, financial accounts, location tracks, and any personal
information of a minor under fourteen) requires separate consent, strict
protection measures, and a prior impact assessment. Important data engages risk
assessment and reporting duties. Any cross-border transfer engages clause 10.
Level 3 use requires the privacy lead's prior approval.

**Level 4 - Decisions about people, and services to the public.** Automated
decision-making with a major impact on an individual's rights and interests;
any generative, deep synthesis, recommendation, or anthropomorphic service
[ORGANISATION] offers to the public. Level 4 engages the full applicable stack -
impact assessment, filing, security assessment, labelling, human review - and
requires approval through the AI Governance Committee.

## 7. Prohibited uses

The following are prohibited for all personnel, at every level, regardless of
tool:

7.1 Using AI to generate or spread content the law prohibits - content
endangering national security or interests, terrorism or extremism, ethnic
hatred or discrimination, violence or obscenity, or false and harmful
information - or attempting to elicit such content from any tool.

7.2 Using AI to produce or spread false news, or fake or misleading depictions
of real people or events; impersonating any natural person; generating or
editing a real person's face or voice without the informed separate consent the
law requires.

7.3 Removing, altering, forging, or concealing AI-content labels - explicit or
implicit - on any generated or synthesised content, or providing tools or help
to others to do so, or passing AI-generated content as human-made where a label
is required.

7.4 Entering state secrets, work secrets, or data subject to export control
into any AI tool; entering [ORGANISATION] trade secrets or third-party
confidential information into a tool not approved for that class of data.

7.5 Using AI to conduct illegal data collection: scraping in a way that
intrudes into others' networks or interferes with normal service operation,
acquiring data by theft or other illegal means, or processing personal
information without a lawful basis.

7.6 Using algorithms to implement unreasonable differential treatment in
transaction terms - including price discrimination based on preferences or
trading habits - or to induce excessive consumption or addiction.

7.7 Using AI tools to make solely automated decisions with a major impact on an
individual - hiring, credit, discipline, significant pricing - without the
human determination clause 11 requires and the individual's rights at
clause 12.

7.8 Providing any personal information or data stored in mainland China to a
foreign judicial or law enforcement authority - including through an AI vendor's
legal-process response - without the approval of the competent PRC authority.

7.9 Directing an anthropomorphic or companion-style service at minors as a
virtual relative, virtual partner, or other virtual intimate relationship;
providing any anthropomorphic interaction service to a child under fourteen
without guardian consent.

## 8. Personal information

8.1 Every use of personal information in an AI tool needs a lawful basis under
the Personal Information Protection Law. For most workplace uses this is
consent, contract necessity, or HR management necessity under lawfully adopted
labour rules; consent obtained must be voluntary, explicit, and informed, and
is withdrawable, with a convenient withdrawal route.

8.2 Minimum necessity applies: the least personal information, for the stated
purpose, retained for the shortest time necessary. Prompts are engineered to
exclude personal information wherever the task permits; identifiers are removed
or the data de-identified before use where feasible.

8.3 Sensitive personal information may enter an AI tool only at Level 3 with
separate consent (or another lawful basis expressly covering it), a prior
impact assessment, and strict protection measures. The personal information of
minors under fourteen is always sensitive and requires guardian consent and
dedicated processing rules.

8.4 Providing personal information to another processor - including an AI
supplier acting as an independent processor - requires notice of the recipient's
identity, contact details, purposes, methods and categories, and the
individual's separate consent; entrusted processing requires the contract and
supervision at clause 13.

8.5 Where [ORGANISATION] uses interaction data from an AI service it provides,
interaction data that is sensitive personal information is not used for model
training without the user's separate consent.

## 9. Impact assessments

9.1 A personal information protection impact assessment is conducted **before**
processing, and the processing record kept, wherever an AI use involves:
processing sensitive personal information; automated decision-making;
entrusting processing, providing personal information to another processor, or
publishing it; transferring personal information outside China; or any other
processing with a major impact on individuals.

9.2 The assessment covers whether purposes and methods are lawful, legitimate,
and necessary; the impact on individuals' rights and the security risks; and
whether the protection measures are lawful, effective, and proportionate to the
risk. Reports and processing records are retained at least three years.

9.3 Providing or entrusting the processing of important data requires a prior
risk assessment on the statutory heads, and important-data holders conduct an
annual risk assessment and report it to the competent authority. The Enterprise
Annex owns the register.

## 10. Cross-border data

10.1 A prompt is a transfer: sending personal information to an AI tool hosted
outside mainland China is a cross-border provision of personal information, and
routing [ORGANISATION] data to an overseas model endpoint moves the data out.
Every AI tool's inventory record states where prompts, outputs, and logs are
processed and stored.

10.2 Cross-border provision of personal information requires one of the lawful
transfer routes - the CAC security assessment, the standard contract filed with
the authorities, or professional certification - unless a statutory exemption
applies (contract necessity for the individual, cross-border HR management
under lawful labour rules, emergency protection of life and property, or
cumulative annual volumes under the exemption threshold, or a free trade zone
negative-list exemption). The privacy lead determines and records the route or
exemption per tool.

10.3 The security assessment is mandatory - regardless of exemptions - where
[ORGANISATION] is a critical information infrastructure operator providing any
personal information or important data abroad, where important data leaves
China, or where the statutory personal-information volume thresholds are
reached counting cumulatively from 1 January of the current year.

10.4 Individuals are told the overseas recipient's identity, contact details,
purposes, methods, categories, and how to exercise their rights against the
recipient, and separate consent is obtained where consent is the basis.
Transfers stay within the purposes, methods, categories, and scale declared in
the assessment or contract.

10.5 Important data is not entered into any AI tool without the privacy lead's
determination that the use either keeps the data in China or has passed the
security assessment. Data [ORGANISATION] has not been notified or publicly
identified as important need not be declared as important data for assessment -
but the determination is recorded, not assumed.

## 11. Human determination

11.1 A decision with legal or similarly major effect on a person - hiring,
termination, credit, significant pricing, eligibility - is made by an
accountable human, informed but not replaced by AI. The human reviews the
inputs and the recommendation, has authority and competence to depart from it,
and records the determination.

11.2 Individuals subject to automated decision-making are entitled to
transparency about it, to an explanation on request, and to refuse a solely
automated decision with a major impact on their rights. [ORGANISATION] does not
present a solely automated decision as human-reviewed.

11.3 Information push and commercial marketing driven by automated
decision-making always offer a non-personalised option or a convenient refusal
route, and recommendation services provide a working switch-off and user-tag
deletion.

## 12. Individuals' requests

12.1 Individuals have the rights the Personal Information Protection Law gives
them against [ORGANISATION] wherever AI processing involves their personal
information: to know and to decide; to access and copy; to data portability on
the statutory conditions; to correct and complete; to delete on the statutory
grounds - including withdrawal of consent; to an explanation of processing
rules; and, for automated decisions, the rights at clause 11.2.

12.2 Requests are received through convenient channels, acted on promptly, and
refusals are explained with the reason. Requests touching AI tools route to the
privacy lead, because prompts, logs, embeddings, and caches may each hold the
individual's data, and deletion must reach them all - where deletion is
technically infeasible, processing stops except for storage and protection.

12.3 Account cancellation and consent withdrawal are honoured without obstacle;
data deleted or anonymised as the regulations require.

## 13. Suppliers

13.1 No AI tool is procured, renewed, or connected without a written agreement
covering: the processing purposes, duration, methods, categories, and
protection measures for entrusted processing; the supplier's security
obligations and breach notification without undue delay; no use of
[ORGANISATION] data for the supplier's own purposes, including model training,
without agreement; no sub-entrustment without consent; return or deletion at
termination; and the storage and processing locations that drive clause 10.

13.2 Suppliers of tools engaging the CAC regimes evidence their compliance
posture: algorithm filing numbers where required, security assessments
performed, labelling capability meeting the mandatory standard, and - for
overseas suppliers - the lawful transfer route their service architecture
implies. [ORGANISATION] verifies filing numbers are displayed as required.

13.3 [ORGANISATION] monitors supplier performance of these obligations; supplier
processing records for provision and entrustment of personal information and
important data are kept at least three years.

## 14. Labelling of AI-generated content

14.1 All AI-generated or synthesised text, images, audio, video, and virtual
scenes that leave [ORGANISATION] - published, sent to customers, or posted to
any platform - carry the explicit labels the rules require for their content
type, and implicit metadata labels are preserved intact.

14.2 Personnel do not remove, crop out, edit away, or overwrite AI-content
labels, and do not strip file metadata from generated content. Where
[ORGANISATION] obtains unlabelled generated content under a service agreement
that transfers the labelling responsibility, [ORGANISATION] discharges that
responsibility before the content is used publicly.

14.3 When publishing through third-party platforms, personnel use the
platform's declaration function to declare AI-generated content, as the rules
require of users.

14.4 Where [ORGANISATION] itself provides generation capability or content
distribution, the Enterprise Annex governs label implementation, metadata
elements, platform verification, and log retention.

## 15. AI interaction disclosure

15.1 Where [ORGANISATION] deploys AI that interacts with people - chat,
voice, avatars - the service makes clear the person is interacting with AI, not
a human, in ways the user can plainly perceive.

15.2 Anthropomorphic services [ORGANISATION] provides additionally implement:
dynamic reminders when over-reliance or addiction tendencies appear; a usage
reminder at each continuous two hours of use; convenient exit that stops the
service at once and is never obstructed by continued engagement; and the
extreme-situation intervention duties in the Enterprise Annex.

## 16. Security

16.1 AI use rides on [ORGANISATION]'s security baseline under the multi-level
protection scheme: access control, encryption and backup of important data,
network logs retained at least six months, malware and intrusion defences, and
vendor products meeting mandatory national standards.

16.2 AI-specific measures apply: tenant isolation verified for enterprise
tools; prompts and outputs logged for Level 3 and 4 uses; retention set to the
shortest necessary; training-data security managed for any model
[ORGANISATION] trains or fine-tunes, with poisoning and tampering defences and
synthetic-data safety evaluated before use.

16.3 Real-identity verification applies where [ORGANISATION] provides
information-publishing or deep synthesis services to users.

## 17. Breach and incidents

17.1 On any actual or possible leak, tampering, or loss of personal information
or data in an AI tool - including at a supplier - personnel report immediately
through [ORGANISATION]'s incident channel.

17.2 [ORGANISATION] takes immediate remedial measures, activates the response
plan, notifies the competent authorities as the regulations require, and
notifies affected individuals - by the statutory contents - unless the measures
taken can effectively avoid harm, in which case authority direction to notify
is honoured. Product and service security defects with national-security or
public-interest implications are reported within the 24-hour rule the
regulations set. The privacy lead maintains the current statement of reporting
duties and deadlines across the instruments.

17.3 Using a tool outside its recorded determinations - a tool used on
decisions about people it was not assessed for, or an internal tool exposed to
the public without the filings - is an incident under this policy whether or
not data left [ORGANISATION].

## 18. Verification of output

18.1 AI output is a draft, not a fact. Personnel verify factual claims,
citations, calculations, and legal or regulatory statements against
authoritative sources before relying on them or sending them onward.

18.2 Output that will be published, sent to a customer or authority, or used in
a decision about a person is reviewed by a person accountable for it.
Generated code is reviewed and tested like any other code.

18.3 Personnel remain responsible for what they produce with AI assistance.
"The model said so" is not a defence to inaccuracy, infringement, or unlawful
content.

## 19. Intellectual property and records

19.1 Personnel respect intellectual property in prompts and outputs: no
uploading of third-party works beyond licence terms, no generation intended to
imitate a specific author's or artist's protected expression for commercial
use, and no use of outputs that infringe others' rights.

19.2 Training data for any model [ORGANISATION] develops has a lawful source,
respects others' IP, and where it includes personal information, rests on
consent or another lawful basis. Data labelling in development follows clear,
specific, operable labelling rules with quality sampling and trained personnel.

19.3 Records that document compliance - determinations, assessments, consents,
filings, logs, labelling records - are retained per the statutory minimums (
three years for assessment reports and processing records; six months for
network logs and unlabelled-content provision logs) and [ORGANISATION]'s
retention schedule.

## 20. Employment uses

20.1 AI used in recruitment, evaluation, scheduling, or discipline is a Level 4
use: impact assessment before use, human determination, transparency to the
people affected, and no solely automated adverse decision.

20.2 Where algorithmic scheduling governs work - orders, hours, pay - the
algorithm respects workers' rights to remuneration and rest, and its rules for
order allocation, pay composition, working time, and rewards and penalties are
established and improved as the rules require.

20.3 Monitoring of employees through AI is limited to what HR-management
necessity lawfully supports, is proportionate, and is disclosed in
[ORGANISATION]'s labour rules.

## 21. Responsibilities

21.1 **All personnel:** use approved tools within their approved levels, follow
the prohibitions, verify output, protect labels, report incidents.

21.2 **The policy owner:** maintains this policy, the tool approvals, and the
training programme.

21.3 **The privacy lead / Personal Information Protection Officer:** owns the
lawful-basis positions, impact assessments, cross-border routes, individuals'
requests, and breach notification. Where [ORGANISATION] meets the statutory
threshold, the personal information protection officer is designated, their
contact details published, and their details filed with the authorities; the
data security officer and management organisation are established where
[ORGANISATION] handles important data.

21.4 **Managers:** ensure their teams use AI within this policy and route new
use cases through approval.

21.5 **The AI Governance Committee:** approves Level 3 and 4 uses and owns the
Enterprise Annex.

## 22. What this policy does not state, and why

22.1 This set is built on the national instruments listed in the research note.
It does not state: sector rules (finance, health, news, publishing,
audiovisual); local regulations of provinces, municipalities, or free trade
zones - including FTZ negative lists, which must be checked for the zones
[ORGANISATION] operates in; the mandatory national labelling standard's
technical detail; or the criminal law dimension. Where those apply, they apply
in addition, and the sources held and not held are stated in the source
register.

22.2 Where a statutory volume threshold, filing window, or deadline is stated
in this set, it was read in the held Chinese text; where a duty is stated
without its number, the number lives in the research note.

## 23. Knowledge, skills and acknowledgement

23.1 Personnel complete AI acceptable-use training before first use and
annually: the levels, the prohibitions, the labelling duties, and the
public-facing line. Roles touching Level 3 and 4 uses receive role-specific
training, including the impact-assessment and cross-border modules.

23.2 Personnel acknowledge this policy on joining and at each material update.
