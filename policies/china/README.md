# People's Republic of China - AI Acceptable Use Policy set

**Status: PUBLISHED - verified against the enacted Chinese texts.** PRC law is
enacted in Chinese with no official English translation; every citation in this
set was read in the Chinese text of the held instruments, and no translation -
official or otherwise - was used.
**Educational, not legal advice** - review by qualified PRC counsel before
adoption.

## Purpose

An acceptable use policy for AI tools in an organisation subject to PRC law,
with an enterprise governance annex, built on the three pillar laws
(Cybersecurity Law as amended 2025, Data Security Law, Personal Information
Protection Law), the Network Data Security Management Regulations, and the six
CAC AI instruments - algorithm recommendation, deep synthesis, generative AI,
content labelling, cross-border data flows, and the 2026 anthropomorphic
interaction measures. Obligations are stated as rules a team can apply;
provision identifiers live in the research note, not the policy body.

Focus 5 of the series (implementing and operating).

## What shapes the China edition

1. **The regime is layered and routed by service type.** No single AI act -
   three laws, one State Council regulation, and six CAC instruments that
   deliberately interlock. A tool can sit inside several regimes at once, so
   every tool is routed through the question: which service types does it
   provide?
2. **The public-facing line is the whole game.** The generative AI measures
   reach services offered to the public in China and expressly exclude
   internal enterprise use. One feature toggle can convert an internal tool
   into a regulated public service - filings, security assessment, content
   responsibility, labelling - so the policy makes public-facing-or-internal a
   recorded determination per tool.
3. **Labelling is one duty spread across four instruments.** Explicit labels
   people can see, implicit labels in file metadata, platform re-labelling,
   and a mandatory national standard - and a prohibition on removing or
   forging labels that binds every user, not just providers. The set states
   the merged duty; the research note holds the map.
4. **The newest AI law in this series is here.** The anthropomorphic
   interaction measures, in force 15 July 2026, carry the sharpest bright
   lines in the set: no virtual-partner services to minors at all, mandatory
   intervention on threats to life, a usage reminder every two continuous
   hours, and a bar on training on sensitive interaction data without
   separate consent.

## The renumbering finding

The 2025 amendment to the Cybersecurity Law inserted two articles - including
a new AI clause at Article 20 - and renumbered most of the law. The
multi-level protection duties cited for eight years as Article 21 now sit at
Article 23, and the penalty ceilings rose sharply. Any document citing
pre-amendment article numbers now points at the wrong provisions; this set
cites the amended consolidation only.

## Open gates (stated, not hidden)

1. **The mandatory national labelling standard is not held** - the labelling
   duty is stated from the instruments; the standard's technical content is
   not asserted, and implementers must record the version they rely on.
2. **Sector, local, and free-trade-zone rules are not held** - finance,
   health, news, publishing, and audiovisual regimes, and FTZ negative
   lists, apply in addition where relevant.
3. **The 2022-2023 cross-border procedure measures are not held** - the 2024
   Provisions prevail on conflict and are held; procedure detail under the
   earlier measures is not asserted.
4. **CII designation rules are not held** - the set states the consequences
   of critical information infrastructure status, not how status is
   determined.
