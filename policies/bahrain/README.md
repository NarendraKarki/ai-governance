# AI Acceptable Use Policy set - Kingdom of Bahrain

**Focus:** Implementing and operating AI governance - policies and oversight
**Status: DRAFT - verification gates open.** See below before relying on this set.
**Educational, not legal advice** - review by Bahraini-qualified counsel before adoption.

## What this is

An AI governance policy set for an organisation under Bahrain's Personal Data
Protection Law (Law No. 30 of 2018) and its implementing Orders - a regime with
features that reshape AI governance: **prior notice** to the Authority before
automated processing, **prior written authorisation** for defined categories
(where silence past the statutory period is a refusal, not permission), and
**criminal penalties** attaching to several duties.

## Why the Arabic matters

Bahraini legislation is enacted in Arabic; English versions are translations
that disclaim being law. Reading the enacted Arabic of Order 43/2022 against
its official translation surfaced five divergences - including a **mandatory
DPIA trigger (visual recording and automatic processing of biometric data)
that the English translation omits entirely**. An organisation working from
the translation alone would get camera analytics and biometric AI wrong. The
research note records each divergence.

## Open verification gates (why this is a draft)

1. **Order 48/2022 may have been superseded in 2025** - the data subject
   rights provisions partly rest on it; the current text must be obtained.
2. Most citations rest on official **translations**; enacted Arabic is held
   and checked for the PDPL (key articles) and Orders 42 and 43 only.
3. The March 2022 adequate-countries record (83 entries, dual-verified) must
   be re-checked for amendments before any transfer position is relied on.
4. A one-page staff guide, matching the UK set's two-layer structure, is
   still to be added.

No provision in this set has been signed off by a Bahraini-qualified lawyer.
