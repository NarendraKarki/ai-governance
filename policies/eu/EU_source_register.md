## 4. Document architecture

[ORGANISATION] supplied an existing policy set as a structural template. Only its
architecture was used: section order, tone, the convention that article numbers live in
the research note rather than the policy body, and the practice of flagging house
standards that exceed statute.

**No legal source from that template was carried across.** Every obligation in this set
was derived independently from the instruments in section 1. Where the template carried
a citation and no equivalent Union instrument was supplied, the clause here is an
unsourced house standard and is labelled as such.

## 6. Sources NOT used, as a matter of method

- **No web content entered any obligation.** One Commission page was supplied and was
  used only to identify which instruments to read.
- **No AI-generated legal content.** Every provision cited resolves to text in a file
  in section 1.
- **No secondary commentary, law-firm briefing, or comparative summary.** The
  commencement position in particular was taken from Article 113 of the consolidated
  text, not from any summary of it.

## 7. The verification that mattered most

The commencement schedule. Before the primary texts were supplied, the position on
whether the high-risk regime applied from 2 August 2026 rested on secondary reporting
which described the amending regulation as provisionally agreed but **not yet formally
adopted**.

The primary texts settle it: **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 was adopted on 8 July 2026 and
published on 24 July 2026**, and the consolidated AI Act at 27 July 2026 carries its
changes as `▼M1`. Article 113 as amended defers the high-risk regime to **2 December
2027** and **2 August 2028**.

Had the set been written from the secondary sources, its central section would have
been hedged where it can now be definite - or definite where it should have been
hedged. The difference between those two outcomes is the whole reason for the rule that
the web raises doubt and only the primary text resolves it.
