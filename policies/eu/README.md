## What this is

An AI governance policy set for an organisation under Union law - the GDPR and
the AI Act as amended by the Digital Omnibus. Two features of the framework
shape it: **some AI practices are prohibited outright** (no assessment, consent
or business case cures them - workplace emotion inference is the one ordinary
proposals hit), and the AI Act **commences in stages** - a substantial part
binds today, while the high-risk regime most people expect to bind does not
yet. The policy's Section 4 states which is which, because planning against
the wrong date is the most common error in this area.

## About the levels of use

The policy scales controls through four levels of use. The levels are
[ORGANISATION]'s own classification - no Union instrument defines them, and
they are distinct from the AI Act's own legal categories (prohibited
practices, high-risk systems, transparency-risk systems). Their boundaries
sit where legal duties begin: Level 3 starts where the GDPR's triggers fire
(personal data, special category data, DPIA-level risk), and Level 4 wraps
the AI Act's statutory high-risk classification for the readiness track. The
level labels are the policy's; the categories are the law's.

## The commencement finding

Secondary reporting still describes the Digital Omnibus as provisionally
agreed. The primary texts settle it: **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 was adopted
on 8 July 2026 and published on 24 July 2026**, and the consolidated AI Act
of 27 July 2026 carries its changes. The high-risk regime is deferred to
**2 December 2027** (Annex III systems) and **2 August 2028** (Annex I) -
a deferral, not an exemption. The transparency obligations are not deferred
and bind since **2 August 2026**.

## Open verification gates (why this is a draft)

1. **Annex III has not been read item by item** - the policy says recruitment
   and employment-management systems are "expected" to be high-risk, and that
   word is doing real work until the Annex is verified.
2. **GDPR is cited from the 2016 OJ text** - the current consolidation should
   be checked before any position turns on precise wording.
3. **Member State law is not addressed** - national provisions on employment
   data, special categories and criminal-convictions data must be overlaid per
   country.
4. **DORA is absent by design** - financial entities need a separate annex
   grounded in Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 and its technical standards; nothing
   about digital operational resilience is asserted here.

No provision in this set has been reviewed by qualified counsel.
