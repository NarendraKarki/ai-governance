# Artificial Intelligence Acceptable Use Policy - European Union

**Organisation:** [ORGANISATION]
**Effective date:** [DATE]  **Version:** 1.0
**Policy owner:** [CISO]  **Data protection lead:** [DPO / privacy lead]
**Classification:** Internal
**Review:** at least every six months, and on material change in tools or regulation

---

## 1. Purpose

[ORGANISATION] permits the use of artificial intelligence (AI) tools to improve
productivity, provided that use protects the organisation's data, its customers and
staff, its intellectual property, and its obligations under Union data protection and
artificial intelligence law. This policy sets the requirements for all such use.

Two features of the Union framework shape this document. First, **some AI practices
are prohibited outright** and no assessment, consent or business justification makes
them lawful. Second, the artificial intelligence regime **commences in stages**: a
substantial part of it binds today, and the part most people expect to bind - the
high-risk regime - does not yet. Clause 4 states which is which, because planning
against the wrong date is the most common error in this area.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, temporary staff, and third parties
who use [ORGANISATION] systems, data, or accounts. It covers all AI tools, including
public and generative AI services, AI features embedded in other software, coding
assistants, meeting and note-taking assistants, media generators, AI agents, and
internal AI systems. It applies on company devices and accounts, and on personal
devices or accounts whenever used for [ORGANISATION] work or data.

Compliance is mandatory. Breaches may result in disciplinary action up to and
including termination.

### 2.1 Which role [ORGANISATION] holds

Union AI law places different duties on the **provider** of an AI system and on its
**deployer**. [ORGANISATION] is ordinarily a deployer: it uses systems built by
others. It becomes a provider, and takes on materially heavier duties, if it puts an
AI system on the market or into service under its own name or trade mark, or if it
substantially modifies a system, or if it puts its name to a system it has adapted.

**Fine-tuning a model, rebranding a vendor's assistant, or embedding a third-party
model in a product [ORGANISATION] sells can change the role.** No such step may be
taken without the assessment in clause 4.4.

## 3. Approved tools

3.1 Only AI tools on the [ORGANISATION] Approved AI Tools list may be used with
[ORGANISATION] or personal data.

3.2 A tool is added to the list only after security and data protection review, and,
where personal data is involved, on the basis of a written processing agreement
binding the supplier to act only on [ORGANISATION]'s documented instructions.

3.3 Use of any tool not on the list - including free or personal-account versions of
listed tools - is not permitted for [ORGANISATION] work or data. Staff who need a
tool that is not listed must request it through [FORM / CHANNEL]; requests are
assessed within [N] business days.

3.4 Staff already using an unapproved tool must disclose this to the policy owner.
Good-faith disclosure will not of itself be treated as a disciplinary matter.

## 4. What binds today, and what lands next

**Read this clause before clause 5.** Obligations under the artificial intelligence
regime commence on different dates. Treating a deferred obligation as live wastes
effort; treating a live one as deferred is a breach.

### 4.1 In force now

- **The prohibited practices** in clause 6.1. In force since February 2025.
- **The staff knowledge and skills duty** in clause 20. In force since February 2025.
- **Obligations on general-purpose AI models**, which fall on the model provider
  rather than on [ORGANISATION] as a user. In force since August 2025.
- **The transparency obligations** in clause 9. **In force since 2 August 2026** -
  these are new, and are the obligations most likely to affect [ORGANISATION]'s
  existing deployments today.
- The whole of the data protection regime, which has bound since 2018 and is not
  staged.

### 4.2 Not yet in force

- **The high-risk regime** - classification, risk management, data governance,
  technical documentation, logging, human oversight, accuracy and robustness, the
  conformity assessment, registration, and the deployer duties that go with them.

  It applies from **2 December 2027** for systems that are high-risk because of the
  purpose they are used for, and from **2 August 2028** for AI embedded in products
  already covered by Union product safety legislation.

  **This is a deferral, not an exemption.** Systems being procured or built now will
  be caught. Clause 4.3 exists so that the deferral is used rather than wasted.

- **Two further prohibitions**, concerning non-consensual intimate imagery and child
  sexual abuse material, which apply from **2 December 2026**. [ORGANISATION] treats
  these as prohibited from today as a matter of policy; see clause 6.2.

### 4.3 What the deferral is for

Any AI system [ORGANISATION] procures, builds or adapts between now and December 2027
that would fall in the high-risk category must be recorded as such at the point of
approval, and must have a documented path to meeting the high-risk requirements before
the applicable date. The Enterprise Annex sets out how this is tracked.

**A system may not be approved on the basis that the obligations do not apply yet.**
The approval must state when they will apply and who owns readiness.

### 4.4 Role change

Any proposal that would make [ORGANISATION] a provider rather than a deployer
(clause 2.1) requires assessment and sign-off by the policy owner and the data
protection lead before work begins.

## 5. Levels of use

Controls apply according to risk. Where the level is unclear, the higher level
applies. These levels are [ORGANISATION]'s own classification for scaling
controls; they are not categories defined in law and are distinct from the
legal categories the artificial intelligence regime defines. Their boundaries
are set where legal duties begin - and Level 4 records a statutory status:
the high-risk classification, ahead of its commencement.

- **Level 1:** No personal, confidential, or regulated data, and output not used to
  make decisions about individuals.
- **Level 2:** Internal non-personal data, or output that informs work affecting the
  business. Data handling (clause 7) and output verification (clause 11) apply.
- **Level 3:** Personal data, special category data, customer-facing output, or
  decisions affecting individuals or carrying financial or safety impact. All
  controls apply, together with a data protection impact assessment and sign-off by
  the policy owner and data protection lead before use.
- **Level 4 - high-risk track:** Any system that would fall within the high-risk
  category when that regime commences. Treated as Level 3 **and** recorded with a
  readiness owner and target date under clause 4.3.

## 6. Prohibited practices

### 6.1 Prohibited by law - no exception, no assessment, no consent

These are prohibited outright. They are not risks the organisation may accept.

- AI that uses subliminal, purposefully manipulative or deceptive techniques to
  materially distort a person's behaviour so as to impair their ability to make an
  informed decision, causing or likely to cause significant harm;
- AI that exploits vulnerabilities arising from a person's age, disability, or
  specific social or economic situation, so as to materially distort behaviour in a
  way that causes or is likely to cause significant harm;
- social scoring - evaluating or classifying people over time on the basis of social
  behaviour or personal characteristics, where the score leads to detrimental
  treatment in an unrelated context, or to treatment that is unjustified or
  disproportionate;
- assessing or predicting the risk that a person will commit a criminal offence based
  solely on profiling or personality traits;
- creating or expanding facial recognition databases by untargeted scraping of facial
  images from the internet or CCTV;
- **inferring the emotions of a person in the workplace or in education
  institutions**, except where the system is intended for medical or safety reasons;
- biometric categorisation that deduces or infers a person's race, political opinions,
  trade union membership, religious or philosophical beliefs, sex life or sexual
  orientation;
- real-time remote biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces for law
  enforcement purposes, outside the narrow authorised cases.

**The emotion-inference prohibition is the one most likely to be reached by an
ordinary business proposal.** Sentiment or engagement scoring of employees from
camera, voice or keystroke data, AI-scored interview analysis of a candidate's
demeanour, and productivity tools that rate staff mood are all within it. They are
not permitted, and no impact assessment cures that.

### 6.2 Prohibited by policy from today

Generating or manipulating intimate imagery of an identifiable person without their
explicit consent, and generating or manipulating child sexual abuse material, become
prohibited practices in law from December 2026. [ORGANISATION] prohibits both from
today. Any capability of an approved tool that could produce such material must be
disabled or the tool removed from the list.

### 6.3 Prohibited by [ORGANISATION]

- any other use that breaches the law or [ORGANISATION] policy;
- entering prohibited data into any AI tool (clause 7.8);
- allowing an AI tool to make a final decision about an individual without human
  review (clause 10);
- using AI to produce legal, medical, financial, or safety-critical advice relied on
  without qualified human review;
- using AI to conduct surveillance or assessment of staff except where separately
  authorised and lawful;
- generating discriminatory, harassing, deceptive, or infringing content;
- creating synthetic media of an identifiable person without authorisation;
- presenting AI-generated output as verified when it has not been;
- circumventing the security, monitoring, or approval requirements of this policy.

## 7. Personal data

7.1 Personal data may be processed by an AI tool only where a lawful basis for that
processing exists and has been recorded before processing begins.

7.2 Personal data collected for one purpose must not be processed in a manner
incompatible with that purpose. Customer or employee data must not be used to train
or fine-tune an AI model unless that use has been separately assessed and approved.

7.3 Only personal data that is adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary
for the task may be provided to an AI tool.

7.4 Personal data must be accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date. Output that
alters or supplements a record about a person must meet the same standard.

7.5 Personal data must be kept in a form permitting identification no longer than is
necessary. Prompts, uploads, embeddings, caches and logs held by an AI tool are within
this requirement.

7.6 A data protection impact assessment must be completed, and approved by the data
protection lead, before processing likely to result in a high risk to individuals -
including systematic and extensive automated evaluation of personal aspects producing
legal or similarly significant effects, large-scale processing of special category
data, and systematic monitoring of a publicly accessible area on a large scale. Where
the supervisory authority has published a list of operations requiring an assessment,
that list is followed.

7.7 AI tools must not be used in any way that prevents [ORGANISATION] from meeting
individuals' rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and
objection. Personal data must not be placed in a tool from which it cannot be
retrieved or deleted on request.

7.8 The following must never be entered into any AI tool: authentication credentials
and secrets; payment card data; personal data outside an approved tool and processing
agreement; special category data and data relating to criminal convictions and
offences without recorded approval and a valid additional condition; children's data
without approval; and any data [ORGANISATION] has no lawful basis to process in this
way.

7.9 Where personal data is processed on [ORGANISATION]'s behalf by an AI supplier, a
written contract must bind that supplier to process only on documented instructions,
to impose confidentiality, to assist with security, breach notification, impact
assessments and individuals' rights, and to delete or return the data at the end of
the service. Sub-processors require prior authorisation.

## 8. Transfers outside the Union

8.1 Personal data must not be transferred to a third country or international
organisation unless the transfer rests on an adequacy decision, on appropriate
safeguards, or on a derogation that genuinely applies to the case.

8.2 **Most cloud AI services process data outside the Union, and many do not disclose
where.** A tool is not approved for personal data until its actual processing
locations are established, the transfer basis is recorded, and any required assessment
of the destination's legal regime has been done.

8.3 Reliance on a derogation - including a data subject's explicit consent - is
exceptional, must be documented case by case, and must not be used to support routine
or repetitive transfers.

## 9. Transparency obligations

**In force since 2 August 2026.** These are duties [ORGANISATION] owes as a deployer,
and some it owes if it is a provider.

9.1 Where an AI system interacts directly with a person, that person must be informed
they are interacting with an AI system, unless it is obvious to a reasonably
well-informed, observant and circumspect person in the circumstances.

9.2 Where [ORGANISATION] deploys an emotion recognition system or a biometric
categorisation system in a case not prohibited by clause 6.1, the people exposed to
it must be informed of its operation, and the personal data must be processed in
accordance with data protection law.

9.3 Where [ORGANISATION] deploys a system that generates or manipulates image, audio
or video content constituting a deep fake, the fact that the content has been
artificially generated or manipulated must be disclosed.

9.4 Where [ORGANISATION] acts as a provider of a system generating synthetic audio,
image, video or text, the outputs must be marked in a machine-readable format and
detectable as artificially generated or manipulated. The obligation does not extend to
assistive functions for standard editing that do not substantially alter the input
data or its meaning.

9.5 The obligations in this clause are separate from, and additional to, the
information duties owed under data protection law.

## 10. Automated decisions about people

10.1 An AI tool must not make a final decision that has a significant effect on an
individual - including decisions on employment, credit, eligibility, or access to a
service - without meaningful review by a person before the decision takes effect.

10.2 The reviewer must be able to understand, question, and override the outcome, and
must have the authority and time to do so.

10.3 An individual has the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on
automated processing, including profiling, that produces legal effects concerning them
or similarly significantly affects them, save in the limited cases the law permits.
Where such a decision is made in a permitted case, [ORGANISATION] implements suitable
measures to safeguard the individual's rights, including at least the right to obtain
human intervention, to express their point of view, and to contest the decision.

10.4 Decisions of that kind must not be based on special category data unless a
narrow permitted condition applies and suitable safeguards are in place.

10.5 Clause 10.1 is a **house standard stricter than the statutory floor**, which
operates by way of a right the individual must invoke. Requiring review before effect
is a deliberate choice, recorded as such.

## 11. Verification of output

11.1 AI output is a draft and must not be relied upon without verification by a
competent person.

11.2 Facts, figures, citations, legal or regulatory statements, and code must be
checked against authoritative sources before use.

11.3 The individual who uses AI output is accountable for its accuracy. For
customer-facing or decision-bearing output, verification must be recorded.

## 12. Use by role and function

12.1 Rules apply according to how AI is used. In particular:

- **Developers:** proprietary or confidential source code must not be entered into AI
  tools other than those expressly approved for code; AI-generated code must be
  reviewed before use (clause 13.3).
- **HR and recruitment:** AI must not be used to make hiring, performance, or
  disciplinary decisions about individuals without human review (clause 10). Any
  tool that scores a candidate's or employee's emotional state is prohibited outright
  (clause 6.1). Systems used for recruitment or selection, or for decisions on
  promotion, termination, task allocation, or the monitoring and evaluation of
  performance and behaviour, are listed in the high-risk category that commences in
  December 2027 - subject to the narrow statutory derogation, which cannot apply
  where the system profiles individuals - and must be recorded under clause 4.3.
- **Security and facilities:** biometric identification and categorisation, and
  emotion inference, are prohibited or tightly constrained. No pilot may be run
  without the policy owner's and data protection lead's approval.
- **Marketing and communications:** AI-generated external content must be checked for
  accuracy, rights, and brand standards, and disclosed where required (clauses 9 and
  15).
- **Customer-facing functions:** see clause 16.

## 13. Security and confidentiality

13.1 AI tools must be used only through [ORGANISATION]-provisioned accounts. Personal
accounts must not be used for [ORGANISATION] work.

13.2 Credentials, secrets, confidential source code, and non-public security
information must not be entered into any AI tool other than as expressly approved.

13.3 AI-generated code and commands must be reviewed before execution. AI output
derived from external or user-supplied content must be treated as untrusted.

13.4 Technical and organisational measures appropriate to the risk must be in place
for any AI processing of personal data, and must be documented.

## 14. Incidents and complaints

Suspected exposure of personal or confidential data to an AI tool, harmful or
materially incorrect AI output that has been acted upon, and suspected breaches of
this policy must be reported immediately to the policy owner and data protection lead.

A personal data breach must be notified to the supervisory authority without undue
delay and, where feasible, not later than **72 hours after [ORGANISATION] becomes
aware of it**, unless it is unlikely to result in a risk to individuals. Notification
later than that must be accompanied by the reasons for the delay. Where the breach is
likely to result in a **high** risk, the affected individuals must also be informed
without undue delay.

**The clock runs from awareness, not from the completion of an investigation**, so the
internal report under this clause must be immediate and is not to be deferred while
facts are gathered.

Individuals may complain to [ORGANISATION] and, independently, to a supervisory
authority. [ORGANISATION] provides an accessible means of complaint and informs the
complainant of the outcome. [DPO / privacy lead] owns this process.

## 15. Intellectual property, transparency, and record-keeping

15.1 Third-party confidential or licensed material must not be entered into an AI tool
without authorisation. Ownership and licensing of AI-generated content must be
confirmed before commercial use, and copyright and trade marks must not be infringed.

15.2 Where AI has materially produced content presented as [ORGANISATION]'s work to
customers, regulators, or the public, its use must be disclosed where required by
clause 9 or where non-disclosure would mislead.

15.3 Records of approved AI uses, lawful bases relied on, impact assessments,
processing agreements, transfer bases, high-risk readiness plans, and verification of
decision-bearing output must be retained in accordance with [ORGANISATION]'s retention
schedule.

## 16. Customer-facing AI

16.1 Where AI interacts directly with customers, the customer must be informed they
are interacting with an AI system (clause 9.1), be able to reach a human where
appropriate, and receive output that meets [ORGANISATION]'s accuracy and fairness
standards.

16.2 Individuals must be given the information the law requires at the point their
personal data is obtained, including the identity of the controller, the purposes and
legal basis, the recipients, retention, their rights, and - where automated
decision-making of the kind in clause 10.3 is used - meaningful information about the
logic involved and the significance and envisaged consequences.

## 17. Fairness and non-discrimination

17.1 AI use affecting individuals must not produce discriminatory outcomes. Where AI
informs such decisions, outputs must be checked for bias and a person remains
accountable for fairness. Suspected biased behaviour must be reported to the policy
owner.

## 18. Monitoring

[ORGANISATION] monitors AI use on its managed devices and networks, including the
tools accessed, the discovery of unsanctioned tools, and records of policy breaches.
Monitoring is proportionate, has a recorded lawful basis, and is notified to staff.
**Monitoring must not extend to inferring employees' emotions**, which is prohibited
(clause 6.1).

## 19. Responsibilities

- All staff must comply with this policy, classify their use, verify output, and
  report incidents.
- Managers are responsible for compliance within their teams.
- The policy owner maintains this policy, the Approved AI Tools list, and security
  requirements.
- The data protection lead is responsible for data protection sign-off, impact
  assessments, transfer bases, complaints handling, and liaison with the supervisory
  authority.
- The owner named under clause 4.3 is responsible for high-risk readiness on each
  system recorded on that track.

## 20. Knowledge, skills and acknowledgement

20.1 Staff dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on [ORGANISATION]'s behalf
must have a sufficient level of AI knowledge and skills for their role, taking account
of their technical knowledge, experience, education and training, the context of use,
and the persons on whom the systems are used. This is a legal duty on [ORGANISATION]
as a deployer, in force since February 2025. It does not require any individual to
reach a specified level.

20.2 Staff must complete AI-use training before using AI tools for [ORGANISATION]
work, and on material change to this policy.
