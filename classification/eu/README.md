**Part 2 of the AI Governance Series.**

duties, or no tier-specific duties - with the post-omnibus commencement dates,
the Annex III list read item by item, a full worked example, and the honest edge
cases stated as such.

## Why this artifact exists

The AI Act's obligations all hang off one question: where does a given system
land in the Act's structure? The popular four-tier pyramid answers it badly - the
tiers are not exclusive, and they do not commence together. This walkthrough
replaces the pyramid with five gates run in order, so that a real system, with
its intended purpose written down, comes out the other side with a defensible
classification and the date its duties bind.

It is written for the people who have to do the classifying: governance, privacy,
security and procurement teams, not litigators.

## Legal baseline and verification

Built on the **consolidated Artificial Intelligence Act of 27 July 2026** -
Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (the Digital
Omnibus on AI, adopted 8 July 2026, in force) - read in the primary text.

Verification date: **1 September 2026**. In particular:

- **Annex III was read item by item** in the consolidated text. All eight areas
  are unamended base text; the omnibus did not touch the list.
- **The commencement schedule was taken from Article 113 as amended**, not from
  secondary reporting, which still frequently misstates it: prohibitions since
  2 February 2025 (two additions from 2 December 2026), GPAI duties since
  2 August 2025, the Regulation generally including the transparency duties
  since 2 August 2026, and the high-risk regime deferred to 2 December 2027
  (Annex III route) and 2 August 2028 (product route).
- The omnibus changes that matter for classification - the safety-component
  tests, the machinery reorganisation, the two new prohibitions - are recorded
  with their markers in the research note.

Primary sources, verification dates, and the one supplied document that turned
out to contain no law at all are in the source register. Corrections are
recorded openly, not silently.

## How to use it

Classify one system at a time. Write down its intended purpose first, then run
the five gates in order. Record the outcome, the role held (provider or
deployer), the decisive feature, and - for anything high-risk - a readiness
owner and the applicable commencement date. Section 11 of the walkthrough maps
the outcomes to the levels of use in this series' acceptable use policies.
