# Part 2 - Classifying AI systems by risk: European Union

**Status: VERIFIED against the consolidated AI Act of 27 July 2026.** Gates that
remain open are stated below, not hidden.

## Purpose

A walkthrough that takes one AI system at a time through the tiers of the EU AI Act
in the order the law requires - prohibited practices, then the two high-risk routes,
then the transparency duties, then the remainder - and ends with a classification
record. It reads Annex III item by item, states every test as a usable rule, works
one example in full (a recruitment screening tool), and classifies twenty further
scenarios in short form.

This is Focus 3 of the series (laws and regulations). It also closes the Annex III
gate left open in the Part 1 EU policy set.

## Educational disclaimer

These are educational demonstrations of AI governance work. They are not legal

be reviewed by qualified counsel before it is relied on. Obligations are expressed

## Open gates (stated, not hidden)

1. **Route A depends on product law not held.** Whether a product requires
   third-party conformity assessment is answered by the Annex I instruments
   themselves. Route A findings are conditional on that check.
2. **Commission classification guidelines** (the provision carved out of the
   deferral) are not held. If issued, they bear on the derogation and on the
   boundary cases.
3. **Member State law** is not addressed.
4. One reading is interpretive and flagged: that a recruitment process is within
   "the areas of workplace" for the emotion-recognition prohibition. The worked
   example gives the fallback finding so the outcome does not depend on it.

## Effect on the Part 1 EU policy set

The Part 1 research note recorded Annex III as not read item by item (Limitation 3).
This part closes that gate. The recommended edits to the Part 1 files are listed in
the research note here, section 6.
